# The owner

_Current as of 2026-10-08. Any agent working here keeps this file: when the owner says something that changes how to work with them, add it with the date, in their words where you can._

The owner of this repository is its only human contributor. They act as product manager and architect and expect the agent to be the lead developer who owns the code. They built the repository between October 2025 and April 2026 with GitHub Copilot, stopped when the models of the day could not see or design a user interface well enough to help with the Explorer, and came back on 2026-09-26 with the Claude 5 models. There is no deadline: "It's okay if it's ready when it's ready."

## What the tool has to serve

Their day job is public-facing, PCI-DSS-secured applications at a mid-sized multi-state legal enterprise: .NET Framework 4.8, WebForms, C#, PowerShell 5.1. That C# estate is where they mean to run this tool, and the sample program under `tests/integration/programs/csharp/estate` is its miniature. The shape, in their words (2026-09-27):

> .NET Framework 4.8 WebForms (but using no PostBack nastiness - just using it as the substrate to do some simple data-specific rendering choices on the pages and letting vanilla JS take over for the parts which update; it's using standard .NET WebAPI 2.N controllers), which talks to a .NET WebAPI 2 API gateway, bridging cloud REST into on-prem WCF service calls, issued out to the onprem WCF service hub (for this specific workload + environment combination), which then matches those operations to the proper WCF service. The WCF service will likely call stored procedures in an onprem MS SQL Server, which itself may perform OraQueries into the (yuck) Oracle database at the center of everything. Entity Framework is typical on the WCF services.

What they want from the tool at work (2026-09-26): "I just want to be able to show non-software people what I do and they can _see it_ the way I understand it. I don't want to keep drawing bespoke diagrams for XYZ questions from the business at work."

## Formatting and visual taste

- **Compose constraints for legibility; projection is negotiable** (2026-09-30). “I'm willing to shed the "orthographic" suggestion if it means legibility in the other design. Compose the constraints needed for the design to work, yeah?” The target is the Local Map's card legibility with the World Map's navigability, and unification may be the wrong answer for authoring versus interpreting. In a walking-depth design, keep directories closed initially; seeing through successive open entrances was disorienting. The owner's Don't Starve example also warns that camera-facing objects plus quarter-turn camera changes can lose bearings. [Projection clarification](../ChatHistory/2026/09/2026-09-30.2.record.md#turn-30), [closed-boundary correction](../ChatHistory/2026/09/2026-09-30.2.record.md#turn-32).

- **Layout must not manufacture debt** (2026-09-30). The owner explicitly accepted “Moving a card must not make the software appear more indebted”: “Wonderful catch. Fully agreed.” Preserve architectural evidence independently of a person's arrangement or camera position. [Source](../ChatHistory/2026/09/2026-09-30.2.record.md#turn-22).

- **Let architecture show through its shape** (2026-09-30). “The design, without attempting to be forceful/judgmental, should emergently show technical debt at first glance from the very shape it produces.” Keep the French Corset's visible internal connectivity and the ideas of backs/undersides in mind, without assuming they require literal camera travel behind a 3D object. The owner identifies accommodating complex connections as the primary reason to consider 3D. They also clarify that “orthogonality” in the preceding turn meant **orthographic projection**, as requested for the World Map, not right-angle routing. [Source](../ChatHistory/2026/09/2026-09-30.2.record.md#turn-21).

- **Minimize unnecessary UI text** (2026-09-30). “Make a real effort to minimize unnecessary text. I absolutely saw evidence of these unnecessary subheadings in your smooth-navigation mockup.” The owner specifically warns against accumulating small text callouts. Apply this across agents and designs: remove decorative or redundant text while preserving useful names, controls and evidence. [Source](../ChatHistory/2026/09/2026-09-30.2.record.md#turn-20).

- **Preserving orientation is foundational** (2026-09-30). The owner explicitly endorsed keeping subject and surroundings understandable while entering a system, following wires, opening evidence and changing screen size: “Consistency of view -- the ability to keep one's bearings as they click around.” They warn that screenshot-only iteration misses sudden hops that a meaningful transition could mediate. “I want to understand our software as it's being built. The Explorer should be my window into it.” [Source](../ChatHistory/2026/09/2026-09-30.2.record.md#turn-16). The March antecedent is in the Copilot-era section below.
- **Keep the detail while improving navigation** (2026-09-30). After trying the orientation probe, the owner liked the movement and Neighborhood minimap, but noted that its file-level information was no richer than the force graph and that wires crossing nodes could make it worse. “What the totality of the Local Map and Membrane Map try to convey are more detailed than that.” The navigation mechanism has a positive verdict; the simplified rendering does not replace those richer views. They offered BMC Control-M's similar node/folder overview as a familiar reference for colleagues. [Source](../ChatHistory/2026/09/2026-09-30.2.record.md#turn-18). In the [second probe review](../ChatHistory/2026/09/2026-09-30.2.record.md#turn-23), the owner found snapping connector routes, unclear directory identity/opening, and an overall regression in navigation, looks and features. Preserve the cumulative strengths of all existing views; a common interface grammar has not earned its place merely by drawing every object as a similar card.

- **Aligned whitespace.** "I personally have a _major_ preference to utilize languages which allow me to use an arbitrary number of spaces/tabs so that I can visually align things which are semantically aligned... I optimize for readability to the human eye." Tables and parallel code are hand-aligned. "Let me know if the linters get in your way. Feel free to adjust so long as it isn't a matter of substantive code quality." Adjust lint and format configuration freely for style; never weaken a substantive rule. (2026-09-27)
- **Cut, don't boost.** "A master audio producer will prefer to cut frequencies they don't like rather than boost frequencies they do like during EQ." Fade the irrelevant; never highlight the relevant. No emoji anywhere.
- **Direction and colour.** They want inputs and outputs colour-coded on consistent sides, configurable is fine, and they are "happy to be shown other designs", including HTML mock-ups. They expected three dimensions for distributed systems: "2 dimensions simply aren't enough to handle the number of things that are mutually related to one another." Refined on 2026-09-28: "3D seems good when your universe is big and your nodes and connections are many. 2D seems good when you're trying to work with UI elements." At the outer scale the left-to-right law is dropped: "We're opening ourselves up to 3D specifically because we think that there is insufficient ordinal space to appropriately show dependency shapes honestly."
- **Games as the reference for the outer scale** (2026-09-28). "Is it possible that we could look to videogames for inspiration?" The feel they want: "the outermost interaction surface feels like plopping pieces down on a board game. Wargames system design haha!" The surface: "A cloud white blank surface upon which you plop isometric-looking markers is great too", or "a deep dark gray black background for those folks". The pieces: "floating cubes in the sky above the isometric ground could be a great way to see these things being wired up"; whether they differ in volume by contents is undecided. Placement: "Try smooth placement first", with snapping as "a slider or checkbox", not either/or. Closed boxes: "we should not be able to see inside the boxes until we zoom in enough that we go to a new view. So long as the transition feels seamless, it's fine."
- **District colours** (2026-09-28). Light blue for cloud and light orange for on-prem: "Those are the conventions I normally use in my diagrams at my firm." On the first board they found "the light mode feels low-contrast, whereas the dark mode feels much much higher-contrast. I get the impression of a 'haze' over the scene in the light mode"; the cure was darker sides, strokes and text on the same white board.
- **Explainers, not tutorials on the screen** (2026-09-28). "the tutorial text in the lower-left corner is unwelcome. A simple question mark element in a corner which allows one to bring up a guided walkthrough or an explainer modal/popover of the UI is fine." Hover panels the same: "too much parenthetical text on the hover over an object."
- **The camera turns like the force graph's** (2026-09-28): "moving mouse right = rotating left is more natural to end users and matches behavior of force-directed graph." Any orbit in the Explorer follows the force graph's convention. The other axis too (2026-09-29): "in the same way I suggested earlier that the right click camera rotation oppose the direction of movement on the X-axis, so too should it on the Y-axis." Drag down and the camera rises.
- **A click pins, a hover peeks** (2026-09-28): "clicks on any system should pin the popover and require dismissing (or change its contents when clicking on another system). The pinned popover element should contain a clickable link to dive inside that system and see its guts."
- **Every claim is a link** (2026-09-29). "Anytime I hover over a system or connection and it tells me it contains/uses/depends on X things, I'm like 'great, why is that not a hyperlink?'. My immediate instinct is to attempt to verify." A panel that states a fact links to where the fact comes from.
- **Design faults under test** (2026-09-29). Told that token labels still collided: "Yep-yep. Any way for us to have playwright test for/detect text overflows or other immediate design fails?" So a visual fault geometry can name, words on words or words cut off, gets a failing test, not a note; `tests/e2e/design-audit.ts` is the instrument.
- **At least two ways to do anything** (2026-09-29). From their game development background: "expect there to almost always be _at least two_ ways to do anything in the program (a stance I'd most strictly apply to navigation; this will tie into my desire to find the provenance of the claims made in the world map text almost without a doubt)."
- **Motion along the wires** (2026-09-28): "I absolutely adore the motion from outputs to inputs across the dangling connectors in your world map demo. I would love that gentle data flow motion on the local map too."
- **Floors** (2026-09-28): domains such as on-prem and cloud might sit side by side or stack, "such that there are multiple 'floors' to the cumulative multi-domain system", so that "this governs that" or "this over that" reads from the picture. "I'm open to pushback on that." Settled the same evening as an arrangement rather than a law, districts side by side or stacked by one click and the reading theirs: "Awesome. Agreed."
- **Light outside, dark inside** (2026-09-29). "I quite like the defaults we have where the world map is bright and the local map is dark by default. I think that is a wonderful juxtoposition and gives one the feeling of inside-versus-outside. That said, I also love variations on light/dark and what they might 'feel like' to any given user. For instance, if a user prefers dark outside and light inside, is the world, to them, empty space and the inner areas are the safety of the warm well-lit indoors?" Offered as instruction: "LLMs substantially lack these capabilities and can benefit from the instruction of how certain designs inspire certain human emotions." So the default pairing stays, a bright board and a dark interior, a person may set the two apart, and the feeling a pairing gives is weighed alongside legibility.
- **Back and forward** (2026-09-30). "the system as-is leaves me _deseparately_ wishing for back/forward buttons (undo/redo?) as I am known to hop around navigating a lot." Every view writes its state with `replaceState`, on purpose "to avoid polluting browser history", so the browser's own Back does nothing.
- **They read designs through exact ASCII diagrams.** By their own account (2025-12-05) they are borderline aphantasic, which is also why the visualizations matter to them; in December they judged a design by seeing it built and tuning it on sliders. They began asking for ASCII drawings of a design on 2026-02-24, and from March 2026 a diagram had to be precise down to pin placement. (Corrected 2026-09-29: this entry had dated the diagrams to December.)

- **Compact, accessible action controls** (2026-10-01). The owner explicitly prefers recognizable icons over repeated visible “Release”, “Pin All” and “Read” button text, with accessible names and hover explanations. Entity names and evidence still need readable text. They liked the analytical probe’s workspace/minimap, while identifying wire endpoints disappearing beneath cards as a defect. [Source](../ChatHistory/2026/09/2026-09-30.2.record.md#turn-44).

- **Never extract agent reasoning, in any harness** (2026-10-01). “Let's make sure we never ever attempt to extract reasoning from any agent trace from any harness.” The owner wants a record of what was said so work can continue smoothly and its decisions/actions can be audited; distillation is not their aim.
