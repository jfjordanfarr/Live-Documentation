## Asked for or loved by the owner

- **A wire that leaves its directory once, and a bundle that splits early** (2026-10-08, [Turn 5 of the October 7 session](../ChatHistory/2026/10/2026-10-07.1.record.md#turn-5)). On the `Unit` bundle of the warehouse scene: "arguably unnecessary directional variation (looks like steps) on the connector coming from the `Unit` symbol of `Unit.java` outward to the downstream consumers"; "If the bundle were willing to split off earlier, perhaps some of the chaos at the far right of the frame could have been avoided"; `Unit.java` "could have paid its exist cost from its directory once and bezier'd its way up and over rather than doing a series of steps that (in my opinion) somewhat distract the eye. (Just wait, you'll make it all bezier and it will be just hell on earth to look at hahaha! Guess we won't know unless we try)". The cause, read from the code that day and not changed: the placement prices a wire's pieces alike, pin to slot, slot to slot and slot to pin, so every monotone staircase between the pin and the root's top row costs the same vertical wire and the steps are a tie the solver breaks as it happens to; and a bundle keeps every wire of its pin through every column they pass together, so it fans out only in the gutter before a consumer's column, while the lane host rule (the deepest directory holding an end of every wire) keeps it inside `model` for two columns because Unit.java is an end of every wire. Two remedies, neither built: the straightening weights of Gansner, Koutsofios, North and Vo, who price the piece between two virtual nodes eight times a piece from a real one so that a long edge runs straight and bends only at its ends, here one lever on the slot-to-slot pieces that the lab could measure; and a bundle allowed to split at any gutter where its wires' lanes would part, at the cost of a slot. "Paying the exit once" is the split plus the straightening.
- **A Local Map that visibly strains: membranes that nudge, a card one can drag, a genuine stabilization** (2026-10-06, [Turn 13, mid-turn](../ChatHistory/2026/10/2026-10-06.1.record.md#turn-13)). The owner: "is there any way that I, as the end user of the software, could see the continuous 'warbling' around the membranes, caused by the strain of attempting to project out and round corners which contain nodes and connectors, desperately trying to contain their contents without overlapping other membranes"; opening and closing directories "would cause a lot of growing and shrinking of these membranes", which must be "well-transitioned" so "the user is not disoriented by the sudden jump in layout"; "Just as the Force Graph does, I want to give off this impression of forces-in-contention", as the 3D graph "lets one actually click and drag around a node, watching the entire software rearrange to accommodate -- it's SUPER slick and surprisingly pedagogical. Knowing the parts of the software which are more knotted and more difficult to tug on is astoundingly useful"; "I'd prefer it not be faked, but genuinely an emergent stabilization that is always evolving"; "so too is the Local Map trying to continuosly shrink-wrap the shape of the software in 2D space." What expanding a subdirectory looks like in the Local Map is undecided (directory pseudo-nodes have been discussed). The agent's reading, for discussion: the Local Map is not a simulation and nothing in it warbles, since its placement is solved exactly in a tenth of a second; but the contention is real in it, as constraints (containment, siblings apart, the neck, the gaps) and an objective (the wires' length) rather than springs. A drag could be one more constraint the exact placement is re-solved under, warm-started from the last solution so it runs at frame rate, the whole picture re-accommodating as the person pulls and the rise in the wires' length felt as the card's resistance, which is the knottedness made tangible and nothing faked; a card dropped at a new place in its column is a new start for the order step, which already takes one. The animated re-layout, a transition between two exact solutions with every card, membrane and wire moving at once, is the foundation of all of it, of the drag as of a directory opening and closing, and comes next after the laces. Whether the membranes should also move when nothing is pulled, a slow breathing, is the owner's eye's call later; the agent's caution is that motion with no cause to read is the staged kind.

- **The forces as one solve, not separate functions** (2026-10-06, the same turn). The owner wished for "independently running functions" optimizing membrane boundaries (a contained sibling's wire never outside its membrane) and cumulative connector length. - **Fewer wires reading backward** (2026-10-06, the same turn). "optimizations to minimize the number of times that a node must defy the left-to-right layout rule". The cycle breaking of Eades, Lin and Smyth already minimizes the feedback set heuristically, each card says how many of its references read back, and the deck's flow row counts backward routes; the exact minimum is the feedback arc set problem, which is hard, and weighing the break by reference count is the cheap improvement.
- **Two more forces for the Local Map's layout, named by the owner on seeing the membranes** (2026-10-06, [Turn 6](../ChatHistory/2026/10/2026-10-06.1.record.md#turn-6), mid-turn). First, "the force directing against wires crossing membrane boundaries for which they are not a member": already measured, as the deck's foreign-directory samples (21,575 to 8,695 on the five files with the membranes; lanes are forbidden a foreign directory outright); not linear in the exact placement, so it would act in the order step, weighing a wire's passage through a foreign membrane in a gutter as a crossing is weighed. Second, "the reduction of connector length which spans _outside_ its containing membrane _if both the source and the target are members of the same membrane_": not measured yet, measurable by the same shape test on the deepest directory holding both ends, and expressible in the exact placement, since the corridor through a gutter is the overlap of two segments and covering both pins' heights is a pair of difference constraints, hard or weighted. "Another ingredient in the mix of forces to tug upon the 2D local map simulation."
- **A lever for how strongly membranes avoid crossing** (2026-10-06). Letting sibling directories' membranes interleave and cross would take the five-file scope's vertical length another twelve points down (45% against the uncrossed membranes' 33 to 38%); the owner is "not 100% averse to membrane crossing, we just need parameterized levers to adjust how strong the aversion to membrane crossing is." The exact placement can only forbid or allow a crossing, so a graded aversion would be a decision in the order step, letting two membranes cross where the length saved passes a threshold, with the placement then holding that arrangement. Decided and built the same night ([Turn 12](../ChatHistory/2026/10/2026-10-06.1.record.md#turn-12)): the page tries the ranking's order, the previous picture's and four seeded shuffles and keeps the cheapest by a weighted score the lab tabulates (`branch-restarts.ts`). - **The reward's shape: linear or spread** (2026-10-06). The placement minimizes the sum of the wires' vertical displacements, which lets a few wires carry a whole bend so the rest sit exactly level; the Force Graph's springs are quadratic and spread a bend across many wires. "The eye may prefer spreading." The owner: "That actually sounds quite pedagogically useful and beautiful." To be answered with pictures after the lane pass, not before. A convex piecewise-linear cost per hop stays exact in the same solver; a quadratic one does not. [Source](../ChatHistory/2026/10/2026-10-05.1.record.md#turn-15).
