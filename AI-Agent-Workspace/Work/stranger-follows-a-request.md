# A stranger follows a request from the World Map down to its files

**For the owner.** This is your own use of the tool: showing colleagues on a shared screen how a payment travels, "the consumer payment portal talks to its API, to the WCF router on-prem over a tunnel, to the WCF service, to the databases". On 2026-11-21 six blind testers were asked exactly that of the estate sample ([the record](../AgentSessionHistory/2026/11/2026-11-21/Probes/blind-playtests/README.md#task-1-estate-trace-the-owners-own-journey)). All six gave the chain of systems in the right order. None named a file, because none went below the World Map, and the two strongest read a staging address as a fault in the payment's path.

**Done when.** Blind testers given that task, and the same question of two public codebases of other shapes, name the pieces in order at the grain of files, and none reports a fault that is not in the source.

**Rests on.** Thirty-two runs by models, on one build, most of them on one sample. Models "live on text" (your caution of 2026-11-19), so a stumble is counted from what a tester did and got wrong and never from what it wished for; a person may stumble elsewhere. The order of the tasks below is the order in which strangers stumbled, most often first, and is a reading of those runs, not a decision of what to build: each is to come to you as pictures of alternatives tried on strangers, the form you asked for on 2026-11-19.

**Waiting on the owner.** Their eye on the Step default, not blocking the work (root recommends With Its Lines; Tuning → 2D Layout → Step). The arrival they meant is Reading, Then Neighbours ([Turn 5](../AgentSessionHistory/2026/11/2026-11-21/2026-11-21.1.record.md#turn-5)); its motion is being measured before it is put to their eye again.

**Alternatives set down by root on 2026-11-21, none built.** Two principles pull here. A *signifier* must be read at a glance, and hue with side is a code a stranger has to learn. A *convention* is already in a developer's head, and it runs against ours: in an import or UML diagram an arrow from A to B says "A uses B", while our wire runs from what is offered to what uses it, as a node editor's does. So a stronger arrow along our direction may make a stranger more surely wrong, and only words are free of the doubt. To be built as one lever on the 2D map and tried on strangers on the estate's two tasks, counted by directions given wrongly and by hedges:

1. *As now*: gradient and sides.
2. *A wire answers*: every wire takes a rested pointer, a press and the keyboard; it then stands out by the others fading and says one line at the wire, the user as its subject, "`PaymentService.cs` uses `PaymentsContext.cs`", with what is used and how many references, in the form of the World Map's panel; a press pins it. This is the standing rule built, and one sentence in place of six vocabularies.
3. *Chevrons along the wire*, pointing the way the gradient runs, from what is offered to what uses it.
4. *Chevrons the other way*, from the user to the used, as a dependency arrow points: against the owner's rule and in the trial only to learn which convention a stranger brings.
5. *Flow on what is pointed at*: dashes moving from offered to user along the wires of the card or wire under a rested pointer, still under reduced motion. For the owner's eye, since a tester sees stills.

**Built as a trial, 2026-11-21, and looked at by root** ([the pictures](../AgentSessionHistory/2026/11/2026-11-21/Screenshots/line-direction/README.md)). *Wire Answers*: every wire has an undrawn band twelve pixels wide that takes a rested pointer, a press and focus; the wire then stands out by the rest fading and a small panel in the World Map's form says, of the estate's wire, "PaymentsContext.cs uses App.config" and beneath it "PaymentsDb · 1 reference"; a press pins it, Escape or a press elsewhere lets go, and a drag begun on a wire still pans. Drawn under the words of a request it would be a suggestion drawn as a fact.

**Alternatives set down by root on 2026-11-22, none built.** Two of the designers' terms apply. *Focus plus context*: what was asked for stays large and what surrounds it stays in sight. *Information scent*: a link says where it leads before it is pressed. One principle under all of them: what is pinned on the board is what is pinned below it, so the way down carries the question and adds nothing of its own. This is [the World Map's open ticket on moving between the maps](../../.mdmd/layer-3/world-map.mdmd.md#open-questions), the owner's own unsettled one ("the transition semantics remain elusive", 2026-10-09), and their question of that day, whether the Local Map of one system should let a person hop to the next "via that 'door'".

1. *As now*: a thing opens as its folder alone.
2. *A thing opens with its doors*: the folder opens with the files that carry its wires pinned, so the files of the neighbouring things stand at its sides in folders of their own; pressing one walks the request a thing at a time, by the Local Map's ordinary step.
3. *A wire opens as its two files*: a wire's panel offers the way down to the file that calls and the file that serves, both folders in view. The smallest honest piece: one hop the board claims, at the grain where it can be read.
4. *A chain opens as its route*: with one thing pinned on the board, a second chosen in "Path to…" asks the way between them, and the answer is the Local Map's path between the two folders, said for what it is.
5. *Opened in place on the board*: a thing's files drawn inside its box. Not put forward: it would be a second drawing of files, and one source and one way of doing each thing is the fifth value.

What each costs: the second to fourth put a link on the board's panels where there is one today, against "almost no words of our own"; the fourth owes words that say a shortest connection is not what a payment does, since the map does not lie; none changes a fact. **For the owner beneath them:** which way a request is told below the board.
