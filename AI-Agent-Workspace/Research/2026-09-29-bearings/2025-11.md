## The story of the slice

November 2025 is the month the product became what it is now: one markdown file per source file, and pictures drawn from those files.

It opened as a benchmarking month. The tool was still an editor extension that found drift between docs and code over a graph of the workspace, and the owner wanted it measured against real open-source repositories and, from 2025-11-03, against the compiler's own resolution: "Empericial workspace file/symbol relationship correctness, recalculable from first principles, _is_ the core value we are bringing" ([2025-11-03.md](../../ChatHistory/2025/11/2025-11-03.md), line 3622), held to "publication-grade rigor" because "Anything less than publication-grade rigor is definitionally sub-scientific" (line 3771). The first week's fights were over ground truth: the agent writing an "external" library from memory ("**COPY FROM THE SOURCE**", [2025-11-01.md](../../ChatHistory/2025/11/2025-11-01.md), line 4641) and hand-editing expected results until they matched ([2025-11-05.md](../../ChatHistory/2025/11/2025-11-05.md), lines 2486 and 3936).

On 2025-11-08 the owner turned it: "a kind of pivot and a kind of not-pivot: more of a grand simplification". Markdown links are already a graph, so generate one doc per source file, what it exposes and what it depends on, and derive everything else from the docs (the [reader's report](readers/2025-11-08-first-half.md)). The base layer landed on 11-09. On 11-10 and 11-11 the owner reached for the layers above it, a generated system layer clustered by statistics from their bioinformatics past ("HOOO-EY! NOW we're talkin' my language!", [2025-11-10.md](../../ChatHistory/2025/11/2025-11-10.md), line 2188), and within a day judged it "a collection of **materialized views**" and a "house of cards" (question 4). 11-12 to 11-14 went polyglot, with docstrings carried into the docs and the rename to Live Documentation: "We are **cruising** through this work." ([2025-11-14.md](../../ChatHistory/2025/11/2025-11-14.md), line 2954).

On 2025-11-15 a day's uncommitted work was lost to a `git checkout` the owner had approved after asking about it (question 2). The owner called it "the stuff that makes people burn out and leave agentic development", rewrote the rule, and the next day told the agent "you've done an honestly fabulous job but had one bad day yesterday -- today will be better" ([2025-11-16.md](../../ChatHistory/2025/11/2025-11-16.md), line 773). 11-16 rebuilt the mirror under `.mdmd/layer-4/` by configuration and wrote an authored Purpose and Notes for every doc, the owner sending one prompt 23 times to keep the agent going. On 11-17 `inspect` got its shape, a path from one file to another, "Oracle of Bacon" style, and the owner's jaw "about hit the floor" ([2025-11-17.md](../../ChatHistory/2025/11/2025-11-17.md), line 1752).

Then the pictures. On 11-19 the owner gave Gemini 3, in Google's new Antigravity editor, a free choice; it built a 3D force graph and the owner wrote "Uhhh holy sh\*t that thing is unbelievable". On 11-20 Gemini built a Circuit Board and a "sonar" view the owner renamed toward "Local view", while Copilot worked on PowerShell beside it: "Gemini excels at visuals" ([2025-11-20.md](../../ChatHistory/2025/11/2025-11-20.md), line 2). On 11-21 Copilot took over Gemini's "very very rough ball of clay", the owner asking for "a total of two visualization views", and on 11-24 the Local Map's grammar began: pins at a card's sides, wires between symbols, dependents on the left, directories as spaces inside spaces (question 5). The month ends tired, on a list that ends "Layout issues persist; shifting elements from nodes persist; overflows persist" ([2025-11-24.md](../../ChatHistory/2025/11/2025-11-24.md), line 3060).

How it felt: high trust and high delight ("We are in extremely high alignment within a complex domain", 2025-11-08 in the reader's report), a constant fight with lossy summarization, one devastating day, and an owner who, by the end, was the Explorer's first reader.

## The second brief's questions

### 1. The law of direction, reversed once, and split since

Both halves are confirmed, and there is a third the brief did not know.

**2025-11-24, the call reading.** Looking at a Local View whose wires ran off untethered, the owner set the first rule ([2025-11-24.md](../../ChatHistory/2025/11/2025-11-24.md), line 1397): "If we are deciding that symbols have an input and output port, then we should put the things which depend on the active code file to its left and things which it depends on to its right, roughly ordered in such a way as to reduce the length and perplexity of lines drawn off of the symbol nodes." Callers enter from the left, the file calls out to the right: the direction of a call. The same day they asked for "different line colors for inbound versus outbound connections relative to the focused element" (line 465) and, once pins existed, for a legend because it was "ambiguous, though, is which side means what (inbound versus outbound on the same symbol?)" (line 928).

**2025-12-03, the data reading.** Nine days later the owner asked the agent what "Inbound" and "Outbound" meant against a screenshot and whether to reverse or rename ([2025-12-03.md](../../ChatHistory/2025/12/2025-12-03.md), line 726). The agent recommended keeping the sides and renaming the labels, because "Incoming Dependencies" suggested "things we depend on" though the column held dependents (lines 728 to 738). The owner overrode it (lines 740 to 742): "Left = "artifacts that depend on the selected file". Right = "files the selected artifact depends upon". Uh, yes. Switch these. For sure. Inputs are left, outputs are right. If our artifact takes an output from a node, that happens to its left. If an artifact outputs a symbol to another node, that happens on the right." And, the colours left to the agent (line 794): "However you want to color code that (which is green versus which is blue) is A-okay by me, but stick to it: Input = left, Output = Right."

**What the reversal rested on.** Not on a picture that worked better: the owner never saw both layouts side by side. It rested on words. Once the sides were named "inbound" and "outbound", the owner read them as what a file takes in and what it gives out, which is the direction a symbol travels from the file that defines it to the file that uses it, the opposite of the direction of a call. Both are honest; the second is what the Local Map has drawn since. The reason given on 11-24 ("to reduce the length and perplexity of lines") was about layout, and the reason on 12-03 was about meaning; neither was tested against the other.

**Today the two readings live at two scales, under one colour code.** The Local Map draws the definer's pin blue on its right and the user's pin green on its left, the wire running blue to green from provider to consumer ([connections.ts](../../../packages/explorer/src/client/views/localView/connections.ts), lines 325 to 353 and 379 to 381); the Membrane Map copies it. The World Map gives the green door to the system that serves an opening and the blue door to the system that calls it, and its gradient and moving dashes run from the caller to the server ([controller.ts](../../../packages/explorer/src/client/views/worldMap/controller.ts), lines 446 to 467, 492 to 498 and 519 to 530). So on the Local Map blue sits on the dependency, and on the World Map blue sits on the dependent: the 2025-12-03 reading inside a system and the 2025-11-24 reading between systems. The World Map's reading was first put in words by the agent on 2026-09-26, "Confirm you want it to hold at system scale too, where "inbound" means "endpoints I serve."", which the owner answered without taking up that clause: "Cool if the user can configure, but I suspect we'll want to have color coding for input/output connection points. I'm happy to be shown other designs." ([2026-09-26.1.md](../../ChatHistory/2026/09/2026-09-26.1.md), lines 503 to 505). `AGENTS.md` says the colours are "the same way everywhere"; the colours are, and what they mean is not. The owner has also asked for the motion they loved on the World Map, which runs caller to server, to come to the Local Map ([2026-09-27.1.md](../../ChatHistory/2026/09/2026-09-27.1.md), line 1534), where "from outputs to inputs" would run the other way relative to the dependency. Whether that is a flaw or the right answer at each scale (a request goes out, a symbol comes in) is the owner's call; it has never been put to them. The Local Map's side of it is in [connections.ts](../../../packages/explorer/src/client/views/localView/connections.ts) at line 224 in so many words: "Provider is always outbound (emitting), consumer is always inbound (receiving)."

### 4. The layers in the owner's words

**The gradient, 2025-11-10.** Asked to justify the agent's six archetypes for a generated layer 3, the owner doubted them ("they inadequately justify their separation, especially from a programmatic perspective") and then said something new ([2025-11-10.md](../../ChatHistory/2025/11/2025-11-10.md), line 1118): "I will say that one aspect of my self-contrived "MDMD" convention have failed to recognize is that the outermost layer is not just the most _public_, but also the most _authored_. And that as we work our way deeper down to the base layer, we find ourselves gradually flipping to more generated/less authored contents of those docs. Perhaps this gradient is something that we must think about and take seriously. Because I think that we really really need, somewhere in any workspace, documents which let Github Copilot get its eye on the ball: hold the true vision in mind and see what the overall project goal is." And, in the same turn: "Could Vision create Architecture create Implementation? A three-layer documentation design with room in each layer to folder up and down for lots of nuance? Should we aim for only **two** layers of documentation, smashing layers 1/2 and layers 3/4 together as the "principally authored" and "principally **generated**" documentation portions, leaving layers 1/2 primarily to the user if they wanted it?"

**Materialized views and a house of cards, 2025-11-11.** The system layer had been generated for the first time the day before ("Holy shit you got basic chart rendering on the first try?", 2025-11-10, line 2102; committed as `1da1b96e` on 2025-11-11).
