### The system-scale facts survey

[2026-09-28-system-scale-facts.md](../../2026-09-28-system-scale-facts.md), 130 lines, committed in `de64d922` with the openings feature it fed, the only one of the three committed together with code.

- **Question.** What exists to parse, borrow or write for the facts the World Map needs at system scale: served openings from contract files (section 1) and from code conventions (2), consumed openings from code and configuration (3), what a system stands on (4), what a person declares (5), infrastructure files (6), and prior art for matching a call to what answers it (7). Its title says it: "what exists to parse, borrow, or write" (line 1).
- **How it was gathered.** "by a subagent with web access" (line 3). Package versions, dates and licences "were read from the npm registry API ... on 2026-09-28" (line 6). The brief the subagent was given is not kept, and the transcript records only that it was launched and reported (see below).
- **How it cites.** A `Source` column in every table, with one to five bare URLs per row, and URLs inline in the judgements. The header claims "a source for every claim" (line 3). It holds for the tables: every row has a URL. It holds less well in the judgements, which carry the survey's own inferences (for example, that the estate's WCF contracts "are C# interfaces and the Web API 2 gateway has no swagger.json", line 20, a fact about the fixture, not the web) without a source, which is fair, since they are the writer's reading.

- **Its header departs from "decides nothing".** Line 3: "The judgements at the end of each section were read by the agent and match what was built that day: a small SQL scanner rather than a library, a hand-written project reader, contract files as a second source, and a declared block that no existing format carries." So the survey's seven "Judgement." paragraphs are recommendations, and the header records that they were followed. See section 2.

## 2. "A survey decides nothing", and the judgements at the end of each section

The survey parts keep to it: the tables and quotations recommend nothing, and each part says so at its head. Every survey then ends its sections with a judgement, and those do recommend, in the first person plural: "We would agree with it" (declared-facts, line 538). The declared-facts survey marks each as "A judgement, not a decision; the owner's answer is the decision" (lines 285, 536, 821), which is honest. The system-scale survey does not: its judgements are the subagent's, unmarked, and its header says they "match what was built that day" (line 3), which makes it a record of a decision as well as of the field. The groups survey marks its judgement "not a decision" (line 228) but was written after the grammar it judges.

The rule in `AGENTS.md` ("A survey records what existed on its date and decides nothing", [AGENTS.md](../../../../AGENTS.md), line 35) is therefore true of the surveys' bodies and not of their endings. That is the useful shape, prior art and then a reading of it, and it is what the owner asked for ("Are they ones that we'd agree with?"). The risk is the one this repository already knows: a judgement the owner later reversed still reads, in the survey, as the survey's conclusion. Three have been reversed and none of the three surveys says so (below).

### Did the board text invent a grammar where one existed?

Yes, and on purpose, though the purpose was never put to the owner as a fork. The five questions the agent brought at 17:07 UTC (transcript lines 1515 to 1519) ask what, where, who, how and what wins; none asks whether to adopt an existing file. The system-scale survey's judgement gives the only stated reason: "What must be written is the declared block of a Live Doc ... because no existing format records how an edge is known" (line 80). That reason does not reach the board as built. The board text records no basis at all beyond marking its own connections `declared`; how an edge is known lives in the Live Docs and the graph, which the board is joined to ([boards.mdmd.md](../../../../.mdmd/layer-3/boards.mdmd.md), lines 41 to 48). A CALM document or a Structurizr workspace could have been the declared half of the same join, and both carry open properties where a basis could sit (declared-facts, lines 253 and 255).

The reasons that do hold are the repository's own, and they are good ones: "Markdown is canonical" and every picture's fact comes from the docs ([AGENTS.md](../../../../AGENTS.md), line 62); the board is "in the Live Doc family" with the same round trip ([boards.mdmd.md](../../../../.mdmd/layer-3/boards.mdmd.md), line 18); positions must be hand-writable in the same file for one-file portability (transcript line 1690); and the owner's demand for the least vocabulary, "make the case to me that it should be any more complex than 'Thing' and 'Connection'" (line 2072), which no surveyed grammar meets. What was lost is only interchange: nothing reads a Backstage catalog, a CALM file or a Structurizr workspace into a board, and nothing writes one out, though the system-scale survey called reading "cheap where a customer already has Backstage YAML or CALM JSON" (line 80).

## Good questions

1. **Should a board read or write an existing architecture format, and was writing a new grammar a fork?** The board text is the repository's own grammar, and the owner never weighed it against CALM, Structurizr or a CycloneDX services list, because the five questions put to them on 2026-09-28 did not include it (transcript lines 1515 to 1519). The one reason the surveys give for a new format, that none records how an edge is known, does not apply to the board as built, since the basis lives in the Live Docs. The good reasons (markdown canonical, one round-trip grammar, two nouns, hand-typed positions) hold; what is at stake is interchange. An import of a Backstage catalog or a CALM file would let someone who already describes their estate open it on the World Map; an export would let a board travel to tools a colleague already has. Why it matters: it decides whether a board is ever more than this tool's file. Who can answer: the owner, from section 3's table.
2. **When the owner reverses a survey's judgement, where is the reversal written?** Three have been (positions in their own file, libyear, kinds from a closed list), and each survey still states the old judgement as its conclusion, with the correction only in the Memory files and the transcript. That is the shape the owner named on 2026-04-01 as "a leakage of stale ideas (or a failure to clean old ideas) from the stateful docs" ([2026-04-01.1.md](../../../ChatHistory/2026/04/2026-04-01.1.md), line 1924). Options: a dated line under each reversed judgement; judgements moved out of the surveys into the design docs, leaving the surveys as pure record; or the rule in `AGENTS.md` made true by dropping judgements from surveys. Why it matters: the surveys are the documents a future agent reads before the next fork on the same ground. Who can answer: the owner, for which form; the agent, for the edits.
3. **Should system-scale edges be measured against the published microservice benchmarks the survey found, before more is built on the estate sample?** The estate sample, its twenty hand-verified edges and its adapter share one author ([claude-code-era.md](../claude-code-era.md), question 1). The system-scale survey found an academic literature with its own ground truth for exactly this problem, a 2024 comparison of thirteen tools with a best F1 of 0.86 (line 113), and says the estate's expected file "already is the oracle shape those papers use" (line 116). It is Java and Spring, which the estate is not, so it would test the matching rules rather than the .NET readers. Why it matters: it would be the first measure of a cross-system edge that no agent here wrote. Who can answer: a measurement, once the owner says whether Spring conventions are worth reading at all.
