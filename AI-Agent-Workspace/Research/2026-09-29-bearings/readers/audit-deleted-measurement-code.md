### 1. The AST accuracy benchmark and its fixtures (`ca655066`, `0de42d28`)

**What it was.** A mocha suite, `T061: AST accuracy benchmark` (`git show ca655066^:tests/integration/benchmarks/astAccuracy.test.ts`, line 73), driven by `scripts/run-benchmarks.mjs` and a manifest of 29 fixtures (`git show 0de42d28^:tests/integration/benchmarks/fixtures/fixtures.manifest.json`). At test time it did not run any analyzer. `evaluateFixture` (lines 173 to 245) loaded two committed files per fixture, `expected.json` and `inferred.json`, and counted true positives, false positives and false negatives between them; the materialized clone it had just made was not read (line 177 computes `root` and never uses it). Its default thresholds were 0.95 precision and 0.90 recall (lines 52 and 53); a fixture's own `thresholds` replaced them (lines 110 and 111), and the aggregate check ran only when no fixture had its own (lines 135 to 149), which, with 16 of the 29 carrying their own, was never.

Where the two files came from:

- `inferred.json` was written by `fixtures:record-fallback`, `scripts/fixture-tools/record-fallback-inference.ts`, which imports `inferFallbackGraph` from `packages/shared/src/inference/fallbackInference` (`git show 0de42d28^:scripts/fixture-tools/record-fallback-inference.ts`, lines 15 to 19).
- `expected.json` was written by `fixtures:regenerate`, `scripts/fixture-tools/regenerate-benchmarks.ts`, by one of three oracles (`git show 0de42d28^:scripts/fixture-tools/regenerate-benchmarks.ts`, lines 317 to 345). For the six SCIP languages it ran the real indexer (`scip-typescript`, `scip-dotnet`, `scip-java`, `scip-python`, `scip-go`, `rust-analyzer scip`; lines 1058 to 1086), converted the index with `scip-to-expected.ts`, then applied a fixture's `oracle.overrides.json`, first its `removals` and then its `manualEdges` (lines 1129 to 1169). For C and Ruby it ran the in-repo scanner and then, in the code's own words, "Union with tree-sitter edges for comprehensive ground truth" (lines 391 and 462), where the tree-sitter edges came from `extractTreeSitterEdges`, which calls `inferFallbackGraph({ seeds })` (line 747): the same function that wrote `inferred.json`. Its comment says so: "This ensures expected.json = oracle ∪ tree-sitter for comprehensive ground truth" (line 691). Despite the name, what went in was the whole inference output, not its tree-sitter part: `inferFallbackGraph` always runs every default heuristic (`git show 0de42d28^:packages/shared/src/inference/fallbackInference.ts`, line 214), and the default list is the tree-sitter heuristic followed by twelve regex heuristics (`git show 0de42d28^:packages/shared/src/inference/heuristics/index.ts`, lines 25 to 46), whose header promises "Tree-sitter runs first and provides 100% precision edges."

Nothing in the gate or the runner re-recorded `inferred.json`: `safe:commit --benchmarks` rewrote the expectations and then ran the test (`git show ca655066^:scripts/safe-to-commit.mjs`, lines 52 to 76), and a `git grep` for `record-fallback` at `ca655066^` finds only its npm script and its own help text. So a run compared fresh expectations with inferred edges last recorded by hand.

The fixtures were of two kinds. Twenty-two were small programs written in this repository in November 2025 and January 2026 (per language `basic`/`basics`, `layered`, `pipeline`, `service`, `analytics`, `modular`, `cli`, `webforms`, and one `rosetta` per language). Seven were real open-source repositories at pinned commits, whose source was never committed: the manifest gave each a `materialization` of `kind: "git"` with repository, commit and include and exclude globs, and an `integrity` block with a SHA-256 over the file set: `sindresorhus/ky` at `5d3684ed` (43 files), `libuv/libuv` at `12d1ed13` (118), `psf/requests` at `61e2240f` (18), `rust-lang/log` at `6e173559` (10), `JamesNK/Newtonsoft.Json` at `e5f67150` (240), `square/okhttp` at `ad97bd3d` (151) and `gorilla/mux` at `db9d1d00` (17). `libuv` ran only in a `stress` mode and Newtonsoft.Json in no mode at all (`"modes": []`), so the default `ast` run covered five of the seven.

- "its ground truth for C and Ruby was the union of a regex oracle and that same tree-sitter path": **holds**, in the code's own words above, and the identical edge sets show the effect. - "its per-fixture thresholds had been lowered as far as 15% precision and 5% recall": **holds as a statement of the manifest** (`rust-log` precision 0.15 at line 320, `go-mux` recall 0.05 at line 864), each with a written rationale. What the file adds: at deletion, eleven of the sixteen floors sat within 0.06 of their fixture's score, which is what a floor set just under the score of the day looks like, and the `python-requests` rationale says so outright: "AST analyzer achieves precision~0.69, recall~0.87" against 0.65 and 0.85 (line 269). - One charge the commit does not make and could have: the benchmark ran only with `--benchmarks` (`git show ca655066^:scripts/safe-to-commit.mjs`, lines 52 to 76, default off), so the ordinary gate never ran it at all; it ran in the manual workflow `.github/workflows/benchmarks.yml` (`workflow_dispatch` only, line 4).

That fixes the one fault that made the old benchmark useless: the new oracle measures the code that ships.

**What is gone** is listed under "What replaced it, and what is gone" below; the short form is: the real repositories, any pass mark, any record over time, and any run outside a person's terminal.

### 2. The C and Ruby "oracles", the SCIP pipeline and the SCIP normalizer (`0de42d28`)

**What they were.** Three kinds of expectation-maker, all under `packages/shared/src/testing/fixtureOracles/` and `scripts/fixture-tools/`:

- `cFixtureOracle.ts` (added 2025-11-05, `4fb18f29`): a scanner of `.c` and `.h` files that resolved `#include` directives, with fallbacks to a unique path suffix and then a unique basename (`git show 0de42d28^:packages/shared/src/testing/fixtureOracles/cFixtureOracle.ts`, lines 442 to 496), and credited a `.c` file with an edge to any file defining a function whose name appeared before a `(` in its bodies (lines 508 to 540), with a hand-written override list on top. Its include pattern, `/#\s*include\s+([<"])([^>"]+)[>"]/g` (line 413), is character for character the one the shipped C adapter uses today ([c.ts](../../../../packages/engine/src/live-docs/adapters/c.ts), line 35).
- `rubyFixtureOracle.ts` (same commit): one regular expression, `/require_relative\s+["']([^"']+)["']/g` (line 302), and nothing else; not `require`, not constants.
- The SCIP path for TypeScript, Python, Rust, Java, C# and Go (from 2026-01-27, `cb24c79b` and `c51d535c`; all six by 2026-02-17, `698a1244`): `regenerate-benchmarks.ts` ran the language's indexer over the fixture, `scip-to-expected.ts` turned the index into file-to-file edges, and the fixture's override file could add edges and remove them. `scip-to-expected.ts` is, in its core, the algorithm today's converter uses: a map from each symbol to the file that defines it, then every non-definition, non-`local` occurrence in another file becomes an edge (`git show 0de42d28^:scripts/fixture-tools/scip-to-expected.ts`, lines 219 to 330; compare [scip-edges.ts](../../../../scripts/oracle/scip-edges.ts), lines 201 to 257). It also read SCIP's relationship records to label an edge `extends` or `implements` (lines 332 to 353).
- `scipNormalizer.ts` (2026-01-27): per language, it dropped an edge when either end was a build output or a dependency folder (`node_modules/`, `obj/`, `bin/`, `target/`, `dist/`, `build/`, `out/`, `__pycache__/`, `.venv/`, `go-build/`, `/vendor/`, `.cargo/registry/`, `generated-sources/`, a `.dll`, or a path starting `../`), and dropped self-references and edges with no source (`git show 0de42d28^:packages/shared/src/testing/fixtureOracles/scipNormalizer.ts`, lines 73 to 254). Nothing in it knew what the analyzer found.

**The reasons given.** "the C and Ruby "oracles" (in-repo regex scanners) and the SCIP normalizer that filtered ground truth"; in the decisions log, "those two languages never had independent ground truth", and the WebForms override that "removed a real SCIP edge ... "to fit the heuristic"" ([architectural-decisions.mdmd.md](../../../../.mdmd/layer-3/architectural-decisions.mdmd.md), line 199).

**Whether they hold.**

- C and Ruby: **holds, and more strongly than stated.** The two scanners were regex scanners; the C one shared its include pattern with the shipped C adapter; and from 2026-01-30 (`ae6e1d46`, "integrate tree-sitter union into expected.json generation") every expectation they fed was also unioned with the inference path's output, which is why the expected and inferred files of every small C and Ruby fixture are identical (group 1). So September did not delete a working oracle for C or Ruby.
- The union was the owner's design, on a premise nobody tested; the January report found this ([2026-01.md](../2026-01.md), question 2 and section 5), and the quotations are repeated here because the code bears on them. In January the owner set the rule: "we _want_ a `scip-{lang}` for all languages for our benchmark fixtures to have perfect `expected.json` files ... ensuring that the union of our heuristics and tree-sitter are equivalent to the union of a `scip` and tree-sitter. Reasonable?" ([2026-01-28.1.md](../../../ChatHistory/2026/01/2026-01-28.1.md), line 4146, owner, 2026-01-28); "`scip-{lang}` and the WASM tree sitter (union of outputs) is considered a gold standard for an "oracle"" in the same message that named the danger, "We've run into past troubles, and still have troubles today, with "grading our own homework" -- relying on heuristics to grade our heuristics." ([2026-01-29.1.md](../../../ChatHistory/2026/01/2026-01-29.1.md), line 987, owner, 2026-01-29); and "That's totally fine if the tree-sitter detects more than the Oracle. That's why we take the union, yeah?" ([2026-01-30.1.md](../../../ChatHistory/2026/01/2026-01-30.1.md), line 4570, owner, 2026-01-30). The premise was a question the owner asked, "Did scip-csharp or the C# tree-sitter ever emit **any false positives**? Because a false negative is fine, so long as we find all the legitimate edges." (2026-01-28.1.md, line 4046). The agent answered from three C# edges it had checked and a Go run cleaned by a stdlib blocklist added that day ("After adding the stdlib blocklist: **0 false positives**", line 4058), concluded "**Neither tree-sitter nor SCIP produced actual false positives**" (line 4060), and proposed more than the owner had asked: "Enhanced Oracle = SCIP ∪ Tree-sitter ∪ (carefully filtered) Regex" (line 4075, agent). The owner said yes to a prototype ("I would! Yes please!", line 4087) and held the premise conditionally: "_if and only if_ the statement of yesterday, that it "produces no false positives, only false negatives" **is true**" (2026-01-29.1.md, line 2405). The code then put the whole inference output, regex heuristics included, on the expected side: the agent's "Enhanced Oracle", not the owner's union of compiler and tree-sitter. From 2026-01-30 (`ae6e1d46`) every heuristic oracle was unioned this way; from 2026-02-17 (`698a1244`) sixteen fixtures moved to SCIP alone and only C and Ruby kept it. September's charge is right about the effect and silent about the origin. The decisions log gives the January union both as the example of ground truth that is "never filtered" ([architectural-decisions.mdmd.md](../../../../.mdmd/layer-3/architectural-decisions.mdmd.md), line 59) and as the reason the benchmark was self-grading (line 66), as the January report noted; line 66 is also wrong in its detail: at deletion the SCIP languages' expectations had no tree-sitter in them (`regenerateScipFixture`, `git show 0de42d28^:scripts/fixture-tools/regenerate-benchmarks.ts`, lines 983 to 1191, has no union). `AGENTS.md` (line 85) and [ideas.md](../../../Memory/ideas.md) (line 41), which say C and Ruby have no indexer to measure them against, describe the same state the Copilot era was in; nothing was lost there.
- The WebForms override: **holds.** `csharp/webforms/oracle.overrides.json` removed `Default.aspx.cs -> Default.aspx.designer.cs` with the reason "SCIP direction differs from heuristic; partial classes link designer→cs not cs→designer" and added the reverse edge by hand, among five manual edges; of that fixture's six expected edges, five were written by hand (`git show 0de42d28^:tests/integration/benchmarks/fixtures/csharp/webforms/oracle.overrides.json`).
- "The SCIP normalizer that filtered ground truth": **overstated.** What it removed were build outputs, third-party folders and paths outside the project, the same classes of file that today's oracle keeps out of every copy ([fixture.ts](../../../../scripts/oracle/fixture.ts), line 10: `bin`, `obj`, `target`, `node_modules`, `__pycache__`) and lists rather than drops when they escape the fixture ([scip-edges.ts](../../../../scripts/oracle/scip-edges.ts), lines 13 to 15 and 105 to 107). The fault that fits "filtered ground truth" was in the per-fixture override files, not in the normalizer. Its patterns were wider than today's, and `build/`, `out/` or `vendor/` would have dropped a real source folder of that name; that is the one sense in which it could hide an edge.
- Did September delete a SCIP pipeline that already did what `scripts/oracle/` does? **Largely yes, for the indexing half.** The same six indexers, invoked the same way (`git show 0de42d28^:scripts/fixture-tools/regenerate-benchmarks.ts`, lines 1058 to 1086; [index-fixture.ts](../../../../scripts/oracle/index-fixture.ts), lines 61 to 106), and the same edge rule. The new oracle's additions are real: the symbols that carry each edge, C# project visibility to split same-named types, Rust crate roots narrowed, documents outside the fixture listed instead of dropped, ambiguity listed instead of guessed, no override file, and, above all, a comparison with the generator that ships. The old pipeline's own additions, now gone: edge kinds (`references`, `extends`, `implements`), sparse clones of real repositories at pinned commits, and the override mechanism, which today survives only as the estate's separate `hand-verified-edges.json`. The decisions log's retirement entry does not say the pipeline was rebuilt, nor that the converter's core carried over; a reader of it would think the SCIP work of January and February was among the self-grading.

### 3. The inference path (`0de42d28`)

**What it was.** `packages/shared/src/inference/`, about 7,000 lines by the audit's count ([2026-09-26.1.md](../../../ChatHistory/2026/09/2026-09-26.1.md), line 102): `fallbackInference.ts` (first added 2025-10-17, `3cf24bee`, "Up to T031 and T054/T056 complete"), `linkInference.ts`, one heuristic module per language, and from 2026-01-29 (`20d9c2a4`) a `treeSitter/` loader and extractor. It began as the engine of the first product, the link-aware diagnostics extension: until February 2026 the extension's `symbolBridge.ts`, the server's ripple and knowledge features and the shared rule engine imported it (a `git grep` at the last commit of each of 2025-11-10, 2025-12-20 and 2026-02-10). The Live Docs generator, the product from November 2025, never did. When the language server and diagnostics went on 2026-02-18, the benchmark scripts were left as its only callers, and so it stayed for seven months.

**The reason given.** "the regex-and-tree-sitter path the benchmark scored. The shipped generator never called it."

**Whether it holds.** **Holds.** At `0de42d28^` its importers outside itself were `record-fallback-inference.ts`, `regenerate-benchmarks.ts` and four files under `packages/server/src/features/knowledge/` that nothing imported. Two things the reason leaves out, both of which explain why no one noticed for so long:

- The authored docs said the opposite. The layer-3 doc for it gave its purpose as "feeding Live Documentation dependency generation" and its resolution order as "**SCIP adapter → heuristic fallback**" since "the Jan–Feb 2026 SCIP migration" (`git show 0de42d28^:.mdmd/layer-3/fallback-inference-heuristics.mdmd.md`, lines 13, 18 and 140). No SCIP adapter ever shipped, and the generator never called a heuristic.
- The work was often done twice. The January Go commit "improve Go heuristic precision for benchmark compliance" (`20d9c2a4`, 2026-01-29) changed `inference/heuristics/go.ts` by 327 lines and the shipped `live-docs/adapters/go.ts` by 128; `bc4c589a` (2026-01-15) added both. So the benchmark's scores were a proxy for the shipped adapters that nobody checked, which is how the audit found "Two analyzers per language" (the same transcript, line 104).

**What replaced it.** The tree-sitter adapters in the shipped path, each measured against the compiler (vision step 2). The tree-sitter loader was written fresh in `605ff092`, not carried over.

### 4. The seven vendored repositories and `fixtures:verify` (`0de42d28`)

**What they were.** Seven real repositories at pinned commits (listed in group 1), whose source never entered git: the manifest held their address, commit, licence, file globs and a SHA-256 over the file set. `fixtures:verify` ran on every `safe:commit` (`git show ca655066^:scripts/safe-to-commit.mjs`, line 79) and, for every fixture with an `integrity` block, cloned it and compared the hash (`git show 0de42d28^:scripts/fixture-tools/verify-fixtures.ts`, lines 161 to 177). The same script ran SlopCop over the diagnostics-era `tests/integration/fixtures/simple-workspace/`, which went in the same commit.

**The reason given.** "the seven vendored fixtures that were cloned from GitHub on every gate run"; "The gate loses fixtures:verify (which cloned seven repositories)".

**Whether it holds.** **Holds as a reason to take them out of the gate**: a commit check that needs the network and seven clones to confirm that upstream files did not change is cost with no signal about this repository. **It is no reason to delete the yardstick**, and the commit gave none. What the clones were for, in the owner's words: "A big big big part of bringing in these third-party libraries for benchmarking is to **find places where our software is failing and explore those deeply**" ([2025-11-03.md](../../../ChatHistory/2025/11/2025-11-03.md), line 321, owner, 2025-11-03); "Every new library we bring in is a rich learning opportunity _if we let it be_." ([2025-11-02.md](../../../ChatHistory/2025/11/2025-11-02.md), line 3009, owner, 2025-11-02); and of gorilla/mux, "Popular, noncontroversial, typical", "an excellent challenging opportunity to grow our software into something more robust" ([2026-01-27.2.md](../../../ChatHistory/2026/01/2026-01-27.2.md), line 4976, owner, 2026-01-27). The day after the deletion, the agent found that "the Rosetta-size programs cannot fail a line scanner" and wrote four new programs "built from the shapes real code takes" ([2026-09-27.1.md](../../../ChatHistory/2026/09/2026-09-27.1.md), line 315): the real code had been in the manifest a day earlier. ## Good questions

Two questions this reading bears on are already asked elsewhere and are not repeated here: whether the oracle should hold a floor ([2026-01.md](../2026-01.md), question 3; [README.md](../README.md), question 6) and what decides when the compiler and the adapter disagree ([2026-01.md](../2026-01.md), question 2). What this report adds to them is under "What adds to or corrects the bearings". These three are new, or sharpen an old one with evidence it lacked.

1. **Should the score of each program be kept in git, commit by commit, as the owner asked in November 2025?** "I would also like to see our benchmark results appearing in our git commits so that we can see gradual improvement (or degradation!) over time throughout commits" ([2025-11-03.md](../../../ChatHistory/2025/11/2025-11-03.md), line 321, owner, 2025-11-03). September deleted them with the benchmark, and `oracle:compare` writes nothing down; its `--json` output ([compare.ts](../../../../scripts/oracle/compare.ts), lines 46 to 65) is the record it could keep. Why it matters: a floor needs yesterday's number, and the owner's wish to watch the repository's "inflation and deflation" over its own commits ([ideas.md](../../../Memory/ideas.md), line 9) is the same wish for the map that this is for its accuracy. Who can answer: the owner, for whether a derived record may be committed at all, since the graph index is kept out of git on purpose ([AGENTS.md](../../../../AGENTS.md), "Documentation rules").
2. **Should the seven real repositories be measured again, outside the gate, and are they a "fixture"?** This sharpens the bearings' question 5 and the January report's question 1. Everything needed is recoverable: each repository's address, pinned commit, licence, file globs and file hash are in `git show 0de42d28^:tests/integration/benchmarks/fixtures/fixtures.manifest.json`, and the inference path's scores on them are a baseline (`git show ca655066^:reports/test-report.ast.md`: requests 0.686 / 0.873, ky 0.566 / 0.903, mux 0.898 / 0.733, okhttp 0.868 / 0.257, log 0.200 / 0.333). Why it matters: it decides whether the next adapter work (Ruby, C, PowerShell, and type references) is judged on code no agent here wrote. Who can answer: the owner, then a measurement.
