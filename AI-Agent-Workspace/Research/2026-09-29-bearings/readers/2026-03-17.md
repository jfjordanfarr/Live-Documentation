## Why the Circuit Board was rewritten

**The owner's diagnosis is from February, not from this day.** The agent chose the Circuit Board from its own list of loose ends (line 109, "Circuit Board progressive disclosure, multi-path visualization, directory Live Docs, universal card component"), arguing that it had been called "borderline useless" because it "saturates the DOM with 568+ nodes" and that "It's the first thing a new adopter sees." (agent, lines 682 and 688). The owner's own words are in [2026-02-24.1.md](../../../ChatHistory/2026/02/2026-02-24.1.md), line 2084, in the chat that ran from 02-24 to 03-01:

> Right now, the Circuit board is borderline useless for me and you. For you, it completely fills the DOM with elements. The shape of the whole codebase down to each leaf lays itself down in the DOM and floods your context window everytime. The user experience is not much better. It abstracts/hides insufficient amounts of complexity. I think the "Circuit Board" could perhaps be redesigned as a treemap (possibly powered by a more performant treemap library/algorithm or we'd need to speed our own up?) which showcases nested directories but those nested directories must be interacted with in some way for them to eventually reveal their inner file contents.

So the treemap, and folders that must be interacted with before they reveal their files (what March called progressive disclosure), are the owner's February asks. The same line goes on: "Perhaps that is where we see directory-to-directory dependencies, and perhaps we even swap out directory wrapping for, say, _cluster_ wrapping", and it offers a second road, taken up in the last sections.

**On 03-17 the owner asked for the view's purpose before its fixes.** "Talk about the pain points that the Circuit Board enhancements should theoretically resolve (yes, the DOM excess is a big one), and further, **what the purpose of that visualization is relative to the other visualizations**. What does the Circuit Board view (or whatever we choose to call it) do for us, and where should we expect it **not** to be used (and thus other visualizations should be used)?" (owner, line 709). Four screenshots of the old view went with it; the agent refers to them (lines 721 to 757) and they are not in the record.

The agent named four pains, which the owner did not dispute:

1. Too much at once: "578 individual file tiles at the same zoom level", and "the zoomed-out view (screenshot 1) is a field of illegible dark rectangles" (agent, line 749).
2. No weight: every file tile the same size and colour (line 755).
3. No focus: clicking a file opens the detail panel "but the treemap itself doesn't react" (line 761).
4. Search that "only filters the sidebar/list" and does not move the board (line 767).

## Design rules the owner stated that day, and comparisons between the views
