## 1. The story of the day in the owner's words

**The shape of the session.** It is one chat of fifteen owner turns, not counting two retries, "@agent Try Again" (lines 94 and 716); the first is housekeeping. The owner opens by asking for the summary of the last session and says in passing: "I'm quite excited to share a bug I've found with the new "Circuit Board" (who knows if we'll keep that name) visualization that was reworked in the last session." (owner, line 44). The agent does the housekeeping (lines 46 to 238), and the design conversation runs from line 240 to the end. Nothing was built that day. What came out of it is a design doc, forward references in fifteen other files (the agent's table, lines 1,419 to 1,436, which it called "All 14 documents", line 1,415), a commit message, a phased plan, a test-first "zipper" plan and a target directory tree; the design commit is `9ee60b60`, made at 02:19 UTC on 2026-03-23.

**What started it: a misreading.** "I'm embarrassed to say that my excitement around a bug in the Circuit Board view was instead some of my own misunderstanding about what the elements on the right side represented (sister directories)." (owner, line 242). The owner had expected, from the agent's words of 2026-03-17, "that some sort of squashing effect would allow even _parent_ directories to be visible onscreen", and read the strip of sibling folders on the right as a bug, where they had wanted folders "squashed-up-against (sister?) or surrounding-like-a-membrane (parent)." (same line). **The word "membrane" is the owner's**, in that line, before the agent used it.

Two more things made the view hard to read, in the owner's account: the page on a 4K monitor, and a belief that the view was drawn on a canvas. "Additionally compounding the difficulty in interpretation of the function of the UI was the fact that we're doing all of this via Canvas, meaning I get very odd layouts and zoomed-out results on my 4k monitor" (owner, line 244). In the same turn the owner described the view they wanted: "if the Circuit Board view is able to handle the kind of "this-in-a-that" membrane-like-engulfing visual surrounding the active directory (such that the active directory is always the same size, and the parents drift into the background at increasingly large sizes, enveloping one another) more easily with DOM stuff than Canvas stuff, I'd be curious to know." (line 244).

The agent read the code and corrected the premise: "Good news — the Circuit Board is **already DOM-based**, not Canvas." (agent, line 280), traced the 4K trouble to a layout surface sized at 1.5 times the viewport (line 301), and named the owner's picture: "a well-studied visualization pattern called a **zoomable treemap**" (agent, line 305).

**The unification, proposed by the owner.** After a turn cut short ("Yeah dangit the cat jumped on the keyboard hahahahaha!", line 344), the owner made the proposal the whole design rests on: "I think the unification of the Circuit Board and the Local Map into the same common structure may take that very form: the zoomable treemap." and "The hope is that, emergently, the points at which the two visible Live Documentation artifacts ("nodes" in my above verbiage) share some common directory is the point at which a single membrane engulfs the two files whose connectivity is being examined, connections between those nodes piercing various layers of membrane to reach one another as needed." (owner, line 344). The same turn named the worry: "I think that multi-hop/BFS search becomes vastly harder to visualize in this unified visualization but I'm curious how far we can take this idea." (line 344).

**Pressure-testing.** The rest of the day is the owner testing the idea and the agent answering. The owner's worries, in order: "circular dependencies, barrel files, that kind of thing. Stuff that would turn our visualization efforts into an M.C. Escher painting." (line 445); whether the pins would be drawn, not only the files (line 545); whether a Local Map-style centre can exist at all ("How do we center such an object if it is defined by the contents inside it?", line 640); and whether C# breaks the idea (line 712). Two of the owner's excitements came in reply to the agent's answers: to the barrel answer, "At first read, this seems an insanely elegant answer." (line 541); to the namespace answer, "WOW. That is an impressive analysis." (line 983).

**The name.** The agent wrote "Membrane Map" first, as a row of a table: "**Membrane Map** (unified Circuit Board + Local Map)" (agent, line 703), with "Two views, not three." (line 706). The owner took it at once: "Membrane Map. I _love_ that name. We could prototype it while keeping Circuit Board and Local Map alive to check our work. I think this is going to be awesome." (owner, line 710). So the word "membrane" is the owner's (line 242) and the name "Membrane Map" the agent's (line 703).

**The high point, and why it mattered to the owner.** C# is the language they meant to use it on: "Aaaaaand also all of the places I currently intent to actually _run_ this software and apply its outputs for genuine real-world benefit are.... C#. And where the vast majority of my experience and expertise is." (line 788), and "(I find it a joy to work in; not all do, but I think it's awesome)" (line 790). After the namespace answer: "This reaches at about the upper limits of what I can visualize, so I will be unable to further challenge it until I see its results. That said, I am as excited about this endeavor as I have been about anything in this project." and "I suspect that I would benefit immensely from this visualization personally in my own work and the idea that I could use my own software in my enterprise at some future date is one of great excitement." (line 1062).

**What worried them.** Beyond the Escher cases: that the agent would carry on past a fork. "As I have said in past conversations, though, please pause and ask me when you run into roadbumps/blocks and fork-in-the-road decisions that should get a PM's (my) attention on." (line 1066), with two outcomes they had seen: "A: Github Copilot writes a workaround or assumption and continues without pausing" (line 1067) and "B: Github Copilot pauses and asks and we vastly improve the design and shortcut work before ever going down the discovered incorrect paths." (line 1068); "Let us please go for option B" (line 1070). And the names of the modes: "I'm not sure I totally love the phrasing of "Browse Mode" versus "Explore Mode", but we can work on that later." (line 1655).

**How it ended.** The owner approved the "planned successor" wording for the documents (line 1181), asked for a commit message and a plan of "the overview-level abstract delta" (line 1442), then for a test-first "zippering pattern" (line 1655), then for the target directory structure (line 1826). The transcript ends on the agent's target tree (lines 2,813 to 2,979), with no owner reply to it.

## 3. The owner on the limits of seeing and designing a UI

- **What a model can see.** "(I deliberately downsize the playwright browser to ~1080p because 4k screenshots totally overhwelm the context window and seem like they are almost not interpretable to the LLM models at all)" (owner, line 244). This is the earliest statement in this day of a working rule about pictures for a model. The agent did not answer it.
- **What the owner sees at 4K.** "I admit that the combination of the zoom controls, the odd element layout, and the rather small fonts (on 4k; everything looks fine at 1080p), alongside the intentions and common design elements that were supposed to ultimately both underlie the Circuit Board" (owner, line 330, cut off by the cat). So the owner's own screen made the view look worse than the agent's screenshots did.
- **The owner's own imagination.** "Because I am borderline aphantasic, it will take me some time to envision challenges to this assertion to pressure test it." (line 541). "This reaches at about the upper limits of what I can visualize, so I will be unable to further challenge it until I see its results." (line 1062).
- **ASCII diagrams, and their limit.** "Much much harder to show in your ASCII diagrams (which are incredibly helpful to me, thank you), but will read misleadingly when read in the future." (line 545). The owner found the drawings useful and warned that they left out the pins, which is what made the idea hard: "`auth.ts` and `session.ts` are card-like visual elements dotted with inbound and outbound pins for each public symbol, and those pins wire to one another. These things can get _really_ hairy, really fast." (same line). The agent's next drawing (lines 574 to 603) added pins and a French Corset.
- **One picture will not do.** "Indeed, it is worth considering that, even in this unified visual representation, there will likely be many many different variants of it that will be desirable for any given purpose." (line 545).
- **Reading the UI wrongly is possible for the owner too.** The day began with the owner's own misreading of the sibling strip (line 242), and a belief, wrong, that the views were canvases (line 244). The owner said so plainly.

## 5. Surprises, and what contradicts AGENTS.md or the Memory files

**Edge bundling was disagreed with on the day it was proposed, and went into the plan anyway.** The agent's "dense cross-cutting" scenario, twenty-three lines between two folders, came with "The fix: **edge bundling at the membrane level**." (agent, line 496). The owner: "I disagree. When we're doing pathfinding, we're starting from a _single_ somewhere and ending at a _single_ somewhere." (line 564). The agent: "My "dense cross-cutting" scenario was a strawman." (line 570). What the owner rejected was the scenario the bundling was built to solve; the bundling itself was never withdrawn. It reappears in the namespace design (line 1003), as an "Edge Bundling" section of the first design doc (`9ee60b60`), as Phase 5 of the plan (line 1581), as Rung 4 of the zipper (line 1708), and as `edge-bundling.ts` and `svg-connections.ts` in the target tree (lines 2,850 and 2,861). So the leakage the owner named on the last day of the era began on its first: a concession made in chat that never reached the documents written the same afternoon.

**The zipper's upper rungs were the jsdom tests `AGENTS.md` now forbids.** Rungs 7 and 8 open with "**Test first**: Given a `MembraneNode[]` layout tree, assert the DOM structure" and "Nested `<div>` elements with correct CSS `left/top/width/height`" (agent, lines 1758 and 1760).
