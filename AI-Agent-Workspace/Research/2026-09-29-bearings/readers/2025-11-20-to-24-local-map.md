## In short

These three days are the Copilot side of the Explorer's birth. The views themselves were born in Google's Antigravity editor, by Gemini 3, on 2025-11-19 and 2025-11-20; on 2025-11-20 Copilot spent the day on PowerShell and only linted Gemini's scripts. On 2025-11-21 the owner handed the prototype to Copilot with a charter of two views, and Copilot rewrote it, first as one script and then as the `client/` and `server/` split the Explorer still has. On 2025-11-24 the Local Map's grammar began: pins on a card's sides, wires between symbols, a colour for each direction, a legend, the test-backed shadow, the three columns, and the rule that the map does not respond to the window. The palette, blue for "outbound" and teal-green for "inbound", is from that day and was the agent's choice; the owner had asked only that the two differ. What the owner kept saying all three days was about layout (too vertical, overflowing, shifting on resize), and what they praised was the test-backed shadow, the legend and the force graph.

## Why the Explorer was written, and each first view

**The Explorer.** None of the three Copilot transcripts says why a picture was wanted; that is in the Antigravity record of 2025-11-19, where Gemini was asked what it would most like to build and proposed a graph ([2025-11.md](../2025-11.md), question 5). By that evening: "Gemini is doing work in the Antigravity IDE on some visualizations. It's going a little rough to be honest." (line 2484). And on 2025-11-24 the reason to keep going: "there is an enormous amount of low-hanging fruit that we can pluck just working our way up to WCAG AA compliance on it. The work is jumping a bit ahead on the roadmap, but the tool is surpringly informative already!" ([2025-11-24.md](../../../ChatHistory/2025/11/2025-11-24.md), line 221).

**The charter of two views**, the owner's, on handing the work to Copilot ([2025-11-21.md](../../../ChatHistory/2025/11/2025-11-21.md), line 142): "I want to have a total of two visualization views for the workspace: the "circuit board" visualization (tree graph? I forget the name of it, but we don't have to call it "circuit board") which allows users to see inheritance relationships, click individual file names, and  explore the local neighborhood at the symbol level. The circuit board visualization should, on hover/click on any of its elements, expand that element to show its symbols and the outbound/inbound connections to them (plausibly via hiding all unrelated nodes, much like the current "Local View"). That is, the "circuit board" view and the "local view" should be integrated together, allowing the user to easily move in and out of local symbol-level detail and global file-level detail." The same turn gives the reason for accessibility, text editing: "if we want users to be able to author skeleton code bidirectionally (or alter a doc and hit a button to send to copilot to implement), we need to expect that users will be interacting with it textually. As such, a final requirement for that high-utility main merged view is WCAG AA Level accessibility compliance."
