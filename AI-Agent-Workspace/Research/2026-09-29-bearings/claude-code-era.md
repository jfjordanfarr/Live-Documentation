## The story of the slice

On 2026-09-26 the owner came back to a repository they had not opened "for months due to some burnout on it" and asked one question: "Can you explore a while and tell me what's the baby and what's the bathwater? You'll see absolute _treasure troves_ of user preferences preserved in this workspace." ([2026-09-26.1.md](../../ChatHistory/2026/09/2026-09-26.1.md), lines 12 to 16, 19:20 UTC.) Four subagents audited the repository and the agent summed them up: everything passed, and yet the accuracy apparatus graded itself, two fifths of the code was reachable from nothing a user runs, the public demo carried the chat transcripts, and the Explorer was "five prototypes sharing one sidebar" (the same file, lines 82 to 128).

The owner's answers turned the audit into a reframe. The tool "feels to me more like tooling to actually, as a human, understand one's own programs"; "I want to see the wires cross"; "I just want to be able to show non-software people what I do and they can _see it_ the way I understand it"; and, plainly, "After all this time and all this work, the truth is that this project is still an idea." (Lines 177 to 189, 22:34 UTC.) The agent wrote `AGENTS.md` and a new vision before deleting anything, and the owner said of them: "I finally saw my vision repeated back to me with clarity" (line 427, 23:21 UTC). Where the two disagreed the owner's argument won: markdown stayed the one representation because "the _simplest_ correct form of a program is the _most_ correct form of a program" (line 271), and the agent withdrew its lean toward a separate data shape (line 322).

Then came the deletion: seven commits between 02:23 and 08:57 UTC on 2026-09-27, the last three of which removed about 92,000 lines (line 966). The owner approved ten planning documents by name ("Read all. Approved.", line 602) and a named list of tools ("Deletion is a joy.", line 501); the rest went under a general grant: "I trust your judgment... I actually want you to have and feel a lot of autonomy in this work" (line 920, 07:32 UTC). Two steering messages from that night became rules: look into "the origin of _why_ files you're deleting were authored" (line 636), and, on seeing mined archive material written down as settled, "So much certainty going in. Hmm..." (line 654).

The engine was rebuilt in a day and a night: a compiler-backed oracle, a sample program in the shape of the owner's estate, five tree-sitter adapters committed between 18:47 and 20:54 UTC, one grammar, one derived graph. The owner read the diffs as they came, stopped one commit ("Wait don't commit that yet. Look how many diffs there are.", [2026-09-27.1.md](../../ChatHistory/2026/09/2026-09-27.1.md), line 406, 2026-09-27 21:51 UTC), and said what they feared: "The thing I don't want to have happen is for the understanding of the system to run away from me." (Line 464, 23:03 UTC.)

The slice turns at 02:26 UTC on 2026-09-28. Asked to explain a probe, the agent drew in text a "new" file view. The owner pasted screenshots of the Local Map and wrote (lines 843 to 845):

> I feel like this textual mockup indicates a genuine lack of familiarity with the visualizations which exist presently. The "Local Map" really _is_ precisely what you drew.

> MAN! Oh man did you not even know about the "French Corset" part of these visualizations?

The agent then looked at every view for the first time, and looking found a wrong wire the oracle could not see. The owner's verdict on the first two days: "Hard-won. This is why I've kept those old chat files around... I suspect there was more baby than anticipated in that odd analogy? Haha!" (Line 913, 03:05 UTC.)

Three probes built by subagents came back "pretty mid" (line 1041); four more rounds against a critic came back worse: "Huh. Dang I did not love those at all... These probes really lack vision. Rather than spinning up a subagent, can you maybe work on this yourself?" (Lines 1161 to 1169, 13:58 UTC.) The agent built the next one itself, in forty-five minutes, while the owner sent six notes into the work (lines 1177 to 1211, 14:11 to 14:21 UTC). The answer: "HOLY SHIT THIS IS SO GOOD. WOW! Yeah this is -- this is exactly the direction I'm looking for." (Line 1258, 15:01 UTC.)

After the board the owner led by questions, not answers: how has the field answered this (line 1549); "Did you know Jonathan Blow ships his games with the full engine tooling inside them?" (line 1736); and, of the board text's five proposed nouns, a challenge to justify more than two (line 2072). The board text and the World Map were in the product by 23:05 UTC. On 2026-09-29 a thing on the board opened into a new folder map, which the owner's second look replaced with the Membrane Map the same afternoon, keeping the Local Map as its teacher (lines 2416 to 2467).

How it felt, in their words: relief ("What a relief it is to use a frontier model on September 26th of 2026", first transcript, line 358), joy in the smaller system ("WOO! Excellently done! It's awesome to just not have to solve a problem at all", second transcript, line 623), and trust given early. Under it runs a second thread: "So much certainty going in", "Sad, but okay" (line 434), the fear of understanding running away, and six plain statements that the agent's words had not reached them (lines 631, 763, 767, 1374, 1390 and 1974).

## Good questions

### 1. When does the tool meet the owner's real estate, and what is it graded against there?

Every system-scale number in September comes from `tests/integration/programs/csharp/estate`, which the agent wrote overnight from one paragraph of the owner's description (first transcript, line 912). Its twenty "hand-verified" edges are in [hand-verified-edges.json](../../../tests/integration/programs/csharp/estate/expected/hand-verified-edges.json), written in the same commit as the oracle, `4c965539`; the hand was the agent's. The adapter that finds 17 of 20 was written by the same agent afterwards. The mechanisms differ, so it is not the tautology the old report was, but the program, its expectations and its analyzer share an author and an idea of what the estate is like. The review stop that would have put the fixture before the owner was one of the three declined. Why it matters: the owner's stated use is to stop "drawing bespoke diagrams for XYZ questions from the business at work", and the diagrams they already draw by hand are ground truth nobody here has seen. Who can answer: only the owner, on their own machine, and then a measurement.

### 2. What are the shipped adapters measured on, besides programs an agent wrote for them?

The audit's sharpest findings came from real code: "Run against real repos, they produced `` `cookies.(` `` from a multi-line Python import and fake Go symbols from example code inside comments" (first transcript, line 104). The pass then removed the only real code the repository measured against, seven clones at pinned commits with compiler-derived expectations (the [decisions log](../../../.mdmd/layer-3/architectural-decisions.mdmd.md), line 203, keeps their names; the commits are only in the deleted manifest). Nothing in either transcript shows a tree-sitter adapter run on code the agent did not write. The owner has asked for this twice in other words: the improvement loop over "a known open-source repo" (second transcript, line 340) and "attempting to hand-verify against a known open source repository cloned into some temp directory" (line 2361). Why it matters: the agent's own finding that "the Rosetta-size programs cannot fail a line scanner" (line 315) applies to any program written to be passed. Who can answer: a measurement, with the new oracle pointed at a clone outside the gate.

### 3. What exactly does the Local Map teach, and where is that written?

The owner's rule of 2026-09-29: "don't get rid of the Local Map yet. It still has a fair bit of hard-earned design intuition and style to teach us", and the Membrane Map takes its name "once Local Map truly is at-least-matched-in-quality" (second transcript, lines 2461 to 2467). [owner.md](../../Memory/owner.md), line 33, reads that as a comparison "picture by picture". The pictures to compare against are not in the gallery: `AI-Agent-Workspace/Screenshots/2026-09-29/` holds the World Map and two of the Membrane Map, and the only portraits of the Local Map are the ones the owner pasted, such as [this one](../../ChatHistory/2026/09/2026-09-27.1.images/2026-09-28-0226-1.png). Set beside [the Membrane Map of the same week](../../Screenshots/2026-09-29/membrane-map-repo-live-docs.png) the distance is plain: one is a lit card with its wires and its tests, the other an almost empty tile with a strip of cards at the foot. The agent's judgement has also swung. On 2026-09-26 it called the Membrane Map "the proof" that one renderer should not morph between scales (first transcript, line 455), which became the vision's "the view does not morph under you" ([vision](../../../.mdmd/layer-1/vision.mdmd.md), line 38); on 2026-09-28 it proposed that the Membrane Map go (second transcript, line 2277); the owner chose it as the inside of every system, and had said earlier that "a fully-zoomed-in membrane map is that-and-then-some" (line 843). Why it matters: it is the next work, and its measure is a taste held in code, in old chats and in the owner's eye. Who can answer: the owner, with both views open; the record for the history.

### 4. Which promises of the vision have a guard?

"It calls no model and makes no network requests" ([vision](../../../.mdmd/layer-1/vision.mdmd.md), line 74). [SECURITY.md](../../../SECURITY.md), rewritten in September, says at line 7 that the Explorer page "contacts no other host". [template.html](../../../packages/explorer/src/shared/template.html), line 8, loads the force graph's library from `unpkg.com`, with no version pinned. The Memory files know it ([ideas.md](../../Memory/ideas.md), line 29); the security statement says the opposite, and the check it offers searches TypeScript only, as the deleted audit did. The owner works under PCI-DSS and wrote "**Security and privacy**" in bold among the goals (second transcript, line 1903). September also showed what a guard does: the design audit was asked for in one sentence (line 2424) and on its first runs found labels on labels and words cut off in the World Map, and two faults in itself ([decisions log](../../../.mdmd/layer-3/architectural-decisions.mdmd.md), line 154). A Playwright check that fails on any request to another origin is the same kind of instrument. "No emoji anywhere in the UI" is another promise without one: [detailPanel.ts](../../../packages/explorer/src/client/detailPanel.ts), line 214, and [pathfind.ts](../../../packages/explorer/src/client/pathfind.ts), lines 376 to 379, still carry them. Who can answer: the code, and a measurement.

### 5. What did the owner want from test evidence, and is the graph enough to give it back?

"Scoping down away from knowing whether an artifact tests another artifact in a workspace? Sad, but okay... I hope to one day regain that information" (second transcript, line 434, 2026-09-27). The agent answered that nothing was lost, and the owner withdrew: "D'oh! Makes sense." (Line 458.) The answer was true of a test's direct imports. The deleted builder (`a2d8cd1a^:scripts/live-docs/build-target-manifest.ts`, the function `expandTargetsWithDependencies`) went further: it followed those imports through the files they import, stopped at barrels so that a test was not credited with modules it never touched, and listed fixtures apart. It did this for TypeScript and JavaScript only. The old Local Map shows what it fed: a row of test names on the card, in the owner's own screenshot. The fact is still derivable, by walking the graph, and that walk is the vision's `reachable` seen from a test. Why it matters: the owner accepted a loss on an explanation that was narrower than the thing removed. Who can answer: the owner for what they want to see; the code for the rest.
