## The instrument

What makes a simple question catch mishaps is the discipline around it, not the question. The criteria I'd hold every test to:

- **The answer exists outside the renderer.** A test measures the picture against the graph: how many of the references between the pinned symbols are legible, never how many wires were drawn. No view can win by drawing fewer facts.
- **It names a mishap we actually hit.** Each test is a historical failure turned into a number: routes that changed shape under a pan, wires passing under cards they do not end at, labels colliding, text cut off, a five-pin neighborhood that grew to forty-two cards, cards turned edge-on, directories open on first sight. A test with no failure behind it is decoration.
- **It discriminates, and we predict first.** Before running, we write the expected number for Local, Membrane, Force and the probe. If all four score alike, the test teaches nothing and goes. If a prediction is wrong, our model of the design is wrong. That is the paper falsification you asked for.
- **It is invariant under what should not matter.** Pan, zoom, theme, pin order, dragging a card. Same counts before and after. This is "moving a card must not make the software appear more indebted" as a test, and it is the one the second Codex probe failed.
- **It has a cost side.** Every abstraction hides facts. A view must report how many it hid and how many steps recover one, so a calmer picture cannot win by silence.
- **It is quantized at a fixed frame.** Counts, steps and pixels at a named viewport and pin set. No weighted scores. Board-game numbers.

The deck I'd start with, one row per view, same five-file pin set, same viewport: legible facts over facts in scope; occlusions, where the Local Map sets the bar; collisions and cut-offs from the audit that already exists; route shape after translation is removed, across six pans; hidden facts and the clicks to reveal each; and one journey with a known answer, counting clicks, the smallest target, the subject's screen displacement between frames, and the viewport error after Back. ## Physical space, abstract space

My answer to when: **position carries meaning only where a person put it.** The board is physical because its arrangement is authored information. Gateway sits there because you put it there, and that is a fact about your estate as you understand it. Nothing the scan produces has such a fact behind it. A computed layout is an explanation, and an explanation may be redrawn for each question. That is the authored-versus-computed boundary from last session restated, and it maps onto physical-versus-abstract exactly.

So neither all physical nor all abstract, and the dividing line is not inside versus outside a system. It is placed versus computed. That also answers the nesting question. You can go in and out indefinitely. The presentation turns physical whenever you reach a layer someone arranged, which a nested board would be, and abstract whenever you reach a layer the scan produced. Two presentations alternate as needed, with no universal renderer.

The atmosphere file you described is the proof that abstract space is required. In any literal space a file every other file uses must sit somewhere, and wherever it sits lies about half its consumers. Abstract space can draw it beside each context that uses it, provided every copy is visibly one identity with one evidence trail.

A transition preserves orientation when one thing refuses to move. The rule I'd test: the subject you acted on stays where it is on screen while everything else reflows around it. The orientation probe did that at file scale and you liked it. The handoff did its cousin, the world contracting into the corner, and you liked that too. Both are cases of never moving the subject and its context at the same time. Smooth or instant matters less than that invariant.

Authoring versus analysis are modes, not necessarily views. Authoring needs stable physical space and writes to it. Analysis needs freedom to rearrange and must never write to the authored layout. They share a screen only if analysis is a lens over the board that cannot touch it, which the pinned panel already is in miniature.
