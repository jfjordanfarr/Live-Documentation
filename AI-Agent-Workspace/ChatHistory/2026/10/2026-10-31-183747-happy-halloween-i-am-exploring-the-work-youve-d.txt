  Gosh, I'm not confident I agree with this either. I understand that a haze is
  liable to emerge at sufficient zoom-out, but a user who is interested in
  seeing the local structure of a handful of pinned objects should be able to
  -- well -- leverage the 3D to help visually intuit things which were
  overcomplex for the 2D local map view. I just don't feel like 3D is kind of
  earning its keep relative to its informationality. 3D Local Map aims to
  helpfully orient the user when things get too complex in the 2D local map. At
  one point in time, we even automatically transitioned between the two at
  specific zoom levels, but opted against it when it felt crappy (the
  transition time is very long and I tripped that switch accidentally more than
  purposely). I personally think we'll need to try a bunch of variations on
  point 3 until we're satisfied.

  >Names as landmarks. Directory names at the island and cluster centres at
  overview distance, revealed on approach as files are. This is the Structure
  repair already next in your order, applied to Connections too.

  Do directory names demarcate island and cluster centers? In the more
  hierarchical mode which aims to show directories (which, as you correctly
  recall, we still have quite a bit of polish work to do remaining on) that's
  the case, but out seeing the emergent structure of the software, I don't see
  why that would be the case.

  >Each island settles around its own centre, and the islands stand side by
  side. Gravity pulls a sphere toward its island's centre instead of the
  origin. The islands get places laid out by size around the largest, and a
  weak pull holds each island to its place. This is your separately

  But if it requires a heavy  hand on our part demarcating such centers, we've
  plausibly overcomplicated. Such things should emerge on their own. Indeed,

  galaxies cluster more together than others are defined by minimizing the
  amount of surprise that would occur by toggling on and off other file types.
