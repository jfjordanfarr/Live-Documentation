  is already measured as the foreign-directory samples, which the membranes just
  halved. Your other one, a wire between two members of one membrane escaping
  it in the gutter, is not measured yet but is cheap to add, and it is
  expressible as constraints on the corridor, hard or weighted. The
  crossing-aversion lever cannot be graded in the exact placement, only
  forbidden or allowed, so it would live in the order step as a threshold.

  Wires reading backward. Already minimized, heuristically, by the cycle

  each cycle at its lightest reference by count.

  pass inside the second step: once the columns settle, each card's rows are
  sorted by where their wires lead, then the columns are swept once more at the
  new heights. It is coupled to the column order, not independent of it.

  comes early, because the starts fall into a few basins. On this repository's
  five files, seeds 1 and 7 give the same picture and seeds 3 and 4 sit beside
  the ranked start. On the chain, seeds 2 and 8 are identical. The measured

  The first caveat is that nothing guarantees the best basin is ever found. The
  order step is a heuristic, not a solver, so "best" means best found. The
  second is that "converged" needs a definition that keeps the picture
  reproducible. If the seeds run in a fixed sequence to a cap, the picture after
  any count is the same on every machine, and the lab can tabulate the whole
  sequence. "Only update when a new best is found" then has a precise form: a
  new start replaces the picture only when its score beats the current one by
  more than the churn cost. That cost exists for exactly this. It is the price
  in wire of moving the reader's eye, and today it is set provisionally at 100
  px per swapped pair because no picture has yet had a previous one.

  C. Ever-continuing search. Feasible, and not what I pushed back on. The
  objection was to showing every start, which is a slideshow of unrelated
  pictures. A sequence of improvements shown as moves is a trajectory, and every
  move has a cause the reader can see. Three conditions make it work:

  - The search has to leave the page's DOM. Today each start reorders the rows
    of every card in the page and measures the pins again, because the rows'
    order depends on the start. That is fine before the first paint and
    unacceptable while someone reads. The lab's card model proves the way out:
    it reproduces the page to the pixel from row heights, so once each row is
    measured once, every start's pin positions are arithmetic. A start then
    costs 30 to 80 ms of pure computation, in idle time or a worker.
  - A move must earn its disruption. The churn cost is the threshold. Moves get
    rarer and smaller as the search runs, which is the convergence you describe.
  - The search must end. A cap on seeds, with the lab setting the cap from the
    tabulated sequence. "Ever-continuing" literally would burn battery for
    nothing after the basins are found.

  answer is the orientation rule you gave on October 2: the card the eye is on
  stays put while the world moves around it. The selected card is held still by
  the camera, and everything else slides.

● Built, gated and committed as 8476f928. The picture now moves. Here is what it
  does, what building it taught, and where it lives.

  What moves

  A change of pins, a closed file, an opened card or a changed setting no longer
  redraws the branch picture. Every card, membrane and lane keeps its element
  and slides from where it stood to where it now belongs, over a Move dial in
  Tuning set to 450 ms by default. Rows that change order slide within their
  card. New cards fade in at their place, gone cards fade out. The wires are
  redrawn every frame, as you asked. The card you last acted on stays exactly
  where it is on screen while everything else moves around it, and a Hold Still
  setting chooses whether hovering a card counts as acting on it or only
  clicking does. With motion reduced in the system, or the dial at zero, the
  picture jumps as before. No text announces any of it; the picture's root
  carries a marker while a move runs, which the tests read.

  Five frames of one move (AI-Agent-Workspace/Screenshots/2026-10-07/README.md#t
  he-picture-moves-later-on-october-7) are in the screenshot README, filmed at
  the dial's longest so the frames could be caught. The clicked card at the left
  never moves. My eye: it reads as one picture becoming another. Two things are
  honest flaws for yours: rows that swap pass through each other mid-move, and
  a card whose height changes snaps to it while its membrane slides.

  What running it taught

  Holding by the pose was wrong. My first hold shifted the camera by the card's
  change of place in the picture. The picture crept upward over several moves,
  because the picture's root shifts inside its container as the picture's size
  changes, and the pose cannot see that. The card is now measured on screen each
  frame and the camera shifted by whatever it drifted. Your own drag during a
  move is left alone, since only the camera's change since the start is
  subtracted.

  The wires' glow was the frame rate. The first moves ran at six frames a second
  headless. The browser's counters put script, style and layout at a third of
  each frame, and a test that switched off the wires' drop-shadow filters took
  the same move to sixty. The glow now goes while a move runs and returns at
  rest. On your machine with a GPU the filters cost less, but the choice stands
  either way.

  A test that watches from outside lies. The spec first sampled the move from
  the test runner's clock and found it over at moments the page said it was not,
  because the runner's own tracing was eating the page's time. It now records
  every frame from inside the page and holds the clicked card within 1.5 px on
  all of them.

  Where it lives

  One new pure module, branch-motion.ts, flattens a scene to a pose, every
  element keyed by what it is, and gives the pose part way between two. The
  branch renderer now keeps a stage across renders and applies poses to it frame
  by frame. The controller remembers the last card acted on and keeps the
  stage. The render module keeps the stage's root for branch pictures and drops
  it for any other. The tuning type, storage, panel and template carry the two
  new settings. Nothing in the layout math changed.

  What the search does

  After the first picture is painted from the starts the order step already

  arithmetic. A start is adopted only when its price plus the churn it would
