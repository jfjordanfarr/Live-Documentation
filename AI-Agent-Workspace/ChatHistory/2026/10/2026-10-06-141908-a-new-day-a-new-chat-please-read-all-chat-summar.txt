  What the browser does and does not constrain

  The browser measures text and paints. Everything else is our arithmetic: the
  ranking, the order, the exact placement, the membrane outlines, the routes.
  Membrane overlap is not a browser impossibility. It is a constraint in the
  placement solve, which keeps sibling directories in their rows of a column
  without interleaving, and a rule in the order step's bands. We wrote it, and
  on October 6 the lab priced relaxing it: letting sibling membranes interleave
  takes the five-file vertical wire down another twelve points, 45% against the
  33 to 38% the uncrossed membranes reach. That is exactly your "little
  non-orthogonality", measured for one rule. The browser's genuine limits are
  legible text size and the screen, and neither is what caps the scores.

  What two dimensions constrain

  A non-planar graph has a crossing floor no drawing can go under, and I cannot
  tell you how far above it we sit. What I can tell you is that the order step's
  crossing count swings 30% between starts of the same algorithm. That spread
  is the heuristic's limit, not the plane's. The plane's floor is below our best
  by an unknown amount.

  Why more levers bought so little

  Today's sweep turned the knobs of one search inside fixed rules: the seed, the
  tie rule, the sweep count. Every large gain so far came from changing the
  rules or the solver, not from tuning: the span-minimal ranking, the exact
  placement, membranes as per-column segments, the restarts. Within fixed rules
  the search is already near its optimum, so the knobs move the score by
  hundredths while the start's lottery moves it from 2.2 to 1.77. The rules that
  remain and could become levers of the lab, each with a measurable price:
  membranes allowed to interleave, a tall rank split into two sub-columns, wires
  allowed to pass behind a card instead of through a lane, a backward reference
  drawn backward, a soft gap between membranes instead of a hard one. That is
  where "a little more optimal" lives, if it lives anywhere.

  Your warning, and what the instruments actually check

  We measure membranes crossed: the deck and the lab count samples inside a
