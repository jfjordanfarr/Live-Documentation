  ## Root cause

  The owner's screenshot is the shipped default, drawn correctly by its own
  rules: the axis is **by step**, not by date, and the ticks are **one per
  day**. That pairing turns the burst inside out. The busiest stretch is drawn
  with the fewest ticks, and the ruler hides the "Sep 2026" label that would
  show where the burst starts. My headless run puts every visible label and
  every tick of the burst within a pixel of the screenshot (for example, ticks
  at 47.5, 52.7, 57.4 and 61.1% of the lane, where the screenshot has x≈697,
  738, 772, 800).

  **1. The defaults never changed. The data did.**
  - `packages/explorer/src/client/persistence/local-storage.ts:74-75` sets
  `history: { axis: "steps", granularity: "auto", playRate: 2 }`. It has been
  the same since the scrubber landed in `7d7a39e6` (2026-10-18), and `git log
  -S` / `-G` show no later change to the axis, the granularity or `TICK_BUDGET`.
  - On 2026-10-18 the bundle carried a history sampled at every eighth commit:
  74 steps (`AI-Agent-Workspace/Screenshots/2026-10-18/history/01-control-open-t
  oday.png`). Since 74 is within the budget of 120 (`tick-model.ts:29`), the
  automatic choice was one evenly spaced tick per step, and "Sep 2026" was
  visible.
  - `a3ee07e5` (2026-10-20) moved the history to format 2. The root then
  recomputed it with the default sampling, every commit: 616 dates (2026-10-20
  record, line 794).
  - The automatic choice (`tick-model.ts:164-169`) now rejects every step (616
  ticks) and picks **days**, because there are 115 distinct days and 115 ≤ 120.
  - So the owner felt a change of axis, but what changed was the tick
  granularity.

  **2. Day ticks on a step axis draw activity backwards.**
  - `ticksAt` (`tick-model.ts:144-160`) draws one tick per day, at the day's
  last step (line 152). `positionOfStep` (`tick-model.ts:90`) places it at
  step/last.
  - The space between ticks is therefore that day's commit count. A busy day
  becomes a wide empty gap; a quiet day becomes a tight cluster.
  - Before Sep 2026: 268 commits shown as 93 ticks (2.9 commits a tick).
  - From 27 Sep 2026: 348 commits shown as 22 ticks (15.8 a tick; 25, 32, 29 and
  23 on 27–30 Sep).
  - The burst actually takes the widest stretch, 43.6% to 100% of the width, yet
  it reads as the sparsest.
  - The blank between 43.4% and 47.5% looks like the lull, but it is 25 commits
  of 27 Sep waiting for their day's tick.
  - The real lull, 2 Apr to 27 Sep, is one step on this axis: 0.16% of the
  width.

  **3. The ruler hides the label that marks the jump.**
  - `rulerOf` (`tick-model.ts:176-192`) puts Feb 2026 at 37.7%, Mar at 40.5%,
  Apr at 43.4%, Sep at 43.6% and Oct at 61.3%. Those positions are correct.
  - `placeRuler` (`scrubber.ts:221-236`, line 233) hides any label that would
  overlap the one before it, so the earliest label of a cluster always wins.
  - At the screenshot's width that hides Mar, Apr and **Sep 2026**. Wider
  (1100–1500 px) it still hides Mar and Sep 2026.
  - So 27–30 Sep's 109 commits sit unlabelled between "Feb 2026" and "Oct 2026",
  and the eye reads that stretch as Feb to Oct. On a step axis a quiet stretch
  always squeezes its labels together, so the label after a gap will always be
  the one lost.

  ## A real defect I found (under the by-date axis)

  **The thumb.** `scrubber.ts:253` places it at its tick's position, and when
  the step has no tick it falls back to `step / steps`, which is the by-step
  position. Under By date with a grouped granularity (automatic → days, weeks,

  fallback happens to be right.
  - No test catches it: the scripted sample is too small to ever group, and the
  2026-10-18 by-date picture had 74 steps, so every step had a tick.

  Prettier

  The survey
  (AI-Agent-Workspace/Research/2026-10-22-typescript-formatting-and-linting.md)
  ran every current tool on real aligned lines from our adapters.
  - No mainstream TypeScript formatter keeps hand alignment. Prettier, Biome,
    oxfmt, dprint and deno fmt all collapse every aligned =, : and comment, and
    their output was byte-identical. Their ignore comments cover only one
    statement each. Prettier has closed every alignment request since 2019, and
    Biome says new formatting options are unlikely to be considered. Only
    clang-format aligns, and it mangles TypeScript.
  - Today, the only thing that actually runs Prettier is format-on-save in the
    dev container. It would collapse alignment in any file saved there; nothing
    in the gate or CI runs it. With our settings it would rewrite 402 of 425
    TypeScript files.
  - The linting side has moved on. ESLint 9 reached end of life on August 6, and
    the import-ordering plugin we use doesn't support ESLint 10. TypeScript 7
    shipped without a compiler API. As a result, typescript-eslint stays on
    TypeScript below 6.1, while Oxlint's much faster type-aware mode requires
    TypeScript 7. It also needs our tsconfig migrated off baseUrl and
    moduleResolution: node, which TypeScript 6 already deprecated.

  My recommendation:
  - No formatter for TypeScript: remove .prettierrc and the format-on-save, and
    keep alignment by hand.
  - Keep lint to correctness on ESLint 10, with typescript-eslint, the docstring
    rule, and an import-ordering plugin that supports ESLint 10.
  - Migrate the tsconfig off its deprecated options, which keeps Oxlint open for
    later.
