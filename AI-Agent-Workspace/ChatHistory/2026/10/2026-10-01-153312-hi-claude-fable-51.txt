  Force graph focus. The bundled library already has what is needed: a camera
  call that takes a target point, a look-at point and a duration, and a
  zoom-to-fit call that takes a node filter. The shipped view in
  forceGraphView.ts (packages/explorer/src/client/views/forceGraphView.ts) never
  calls either and exposes only a render function. Focusing a file means
  finding the node in the live graph data, where the simulation writes its
  coordinates, placing the camera on the ray from the origin through that node
  at a fixed distance, and animating there with the node as the look-at. Framing
  a neighborhood is the zoom-to-fit call with a filter of the node and its
  neighbors. One wrinkle: positions keep drifting while the simulation cools, so
  a focus on page load has to wait for the engine-stop event or re-aim each
  tick. It is the same "camera travels, subject stays put" move the orientation
  probe made, which is why it transfers: a focus method on the view, wired to
  the sidebar selection and to the URL state that Back already carries.

  The instrument

  What makes a simple question catch mishaps is the discipline around it, not
  the question. The criteria I'd hold every test to:

  - The answer exists outside the renderer. A test measures the picture against
    the graph: how many of the references between the pinned symbols are
    legible, never how many wires were drawn. No view can win by drawing fewer
    facts.
  - It names a mishap we actually hit. Each test is a historical failure turned
    into a number: routes that changed shape under a pan, wires passing under
    cards they do not end at, labels colliding, text cut off, a five-pin
    neighborhood that grew to forty-two cards, cards turned edge-on, directories
    open on first sight. A test with no failure behind it is decoration.
  - It discriminates, and we predict first. Before running, we write the
    expected number for Local, Membrane, Force and the probe. If all four score
    alike, the test teaches nothing and goes. If a prediction is wrong, our
    model of the design is wrong. That is the paper falsification you asked for.
  - It is invariant under what should not matter. Pan, zoom, theme, pin order,
    dragging a card. Same counts before and after. This is "moving a card must
    not make the software appear more indebted" as a test, and it is the one the
    second Codex probe failed.
  - It has a cost side. Every abstraction hides facts. A view must report how
    many it hid and how many steps recover one, so a calmer picture cannot win
    by silence.
  - It is quantized at a fixed frame. Counts, steps and pixels at a named
    viewport and pin set. No weighted scores. Board-game numbers.

  The deck I'd start with, one row per view, same five-file pin set, same
  viewport: legible facts over facts in scope; occlusions, where the Local Map
  sets the bar; collisions and cut-offs from the audit that already exists;

  Physical space, abstract space

  My answer to when: position carries meaning only where a person put it. The
  board is physical because its arrangement is authored information. Gateway
  sits there because you put it there, and that is a fact about your estate as
  you understand it. Nothing the scan produces has such a fact behind it. A
  computed layout is an explanation, and an explanation may be redrawn for each
  question. That is the authored-versus-computed boundary from last session
  restated, and it maps onto physical-versus-abstract exactly.

  So neither all physical nor all abstract, and the dividing line is not inside
  versus outside a system. It is placed versus computed. That also answers the
  nesting question. You can go in and out indefinitely. The presentation turns
  physical whenever you reach a layer someone arranged, which a nested board
  would be, and abstract whenever you reach a layer the scan produced. Two
  presentations alternate as needed, with no universal renderer.

  The atmosphere file you described is the proof that abstract space is
  required. In any literal space a file every other file uses must sit
  somewhere, and wherever it sits lies about half its consumers. Abstract space
  can draw it beside each context that uses it, provided every copy is visibly
  one identity with one evidence trail.

  A transition preserves orientation when one thing refuses to move. The rule
  I'd test: the subject you acted on stays where it is on screen while
  everything else reflows around it. The orientation probe did that at file
  scale and you liked it. The handoff did its cousin, the world contracting into
  the corner, and you liked that too. Both are cases of never moving the
  subject and its context at the same time. Smooth or instant matters less than
  that invariant.

  Authoring versus analysis are modes, not necessarily views. Authoring needs
  stable physical space and writes to it. Analysis needs freedom to rearrange
  and must never write to the authored layout. They share a screen only if
  analysis is a lens over the board that cannot touch it, which the pinned panel
  already is in miniature.

  Directions that are different in kind from last session, ranked by what they
  would tell us:

  - Subject-fixed concentric explanation. The pinned thing sits at the center
    and never moves. Rings of distance hold full cards with left-in, right-out
    kept per card and not globally. Cycles and multiple branches live naturally
    in rings. It is the Force Graph's idea in two dimensions with real cards,
    and the Local Map's cards without the single axis. Untested.
  - Force-positioned full cards with pins as anchors. Your suggestion. Cards
    repel, wires are springs between pin positions, the subject is held at
    center. The instrument would show at once whether readable cards survive the
    forces.
  - A matrix as the survey. Rows offer, columns use, a cell is a reference.

  - The handoff at every boundary. Whatever you enter becomes the frame and
    whatever you left docks as context, any depth, nested boards included. This
    is the architecture question rather than a rendering, and it decides what
    the subject-stays-put rule applies to.

  Physical space, abstract space

  My answer to when: position carries meaning only where a person put it. The
  board is physical because its arrangement is authored information. Gateway
  sits there because you put it there, and that is a fact about your estate as
  you understand it. Nothing the scan produces has such a fact behind it. A
  computed layout is an explanation, and an explanation may be redrawn for each
  question. That is the authored-versus-computed boundary from last session
  restated, and it maps onto physical-versus-abstract exactly.

  So neither all physical nor all abstract, and the dividing line is not inside
  versus outside a system. It is placed versus computed. That also answers the
  nesting question. You can go in and out indefinitely. The presentation turns
  physical whenever you reach a layer someone arranged, which a nested board
  would be, and abstract whenever you reach a layer the scan produced. Two
  presentations alternate as needed, with no universal renderer.

  The atmosphere file you described is the proof that abstract space is
  required. In any literal space a file every other file uses must sit
  somewhere, and wherever it sits lies about half its consumers. Abstract space
  can draw it beside each context that uses it, provided every copy is visibly
  one identity with one evidence trail.

  A transition preserves orientation when one thing refuses to move. The rule
  I'd test: the subject you acted on stays where it is on screen while
  everything else reflows around it. The orientation probe did that at file
  scale and you liked it. The handoff did its cousin, the world contracting into
  the corner, and you liked that too. Both are cases of never moving the
  subject and its context at the same time. Smooth or instant matters less than
  that invariant.

  Authoring versus analysis are modes, not necessarily views. Authoring needs
  stable physical space and writes to it. Analysis needs freedom to rearrange
  and must never write to the authored layout. They share a screen only if
  analysis is a lens over the board that cannot touch it, which the pinned panel
  already is in miniature.

  ### Summary

  1. **Settled:** a folder is drawn one level open with closed subfolders that
  carry counts on their boundary, and directories are closed on first sight.
