  What went wrong with the sketch

  Measured against your sentence, it failed three ways:

  - It was not the application. A mock page cannot show you your own screen
    changing.
  - The colour picker took over. It is a window of the browser's that covers the
    picture and closes, so every second thought means opening it again.
    Designers call that a modal control. What you described is a modeless one:
    it stays open beside the picture, and each press shows at once (a live
    preview).
  - One press changed a map you were not looking at. Standing in the Local Map,
    you cannot see what a whole-app theme did to the World Map.

  The third one is what overturns the single theme, and it gives a rule for
  sorting any setting: a control should change what you can see while you use
  it.

  Where that leaves the design

  - Local to a view: its ground, light or dark. You change it in the view, you
    see it, you press again to go back. The World Map already works this way
    with its Dark board button. The Local Map gets the same button.
  - Global: the palette of meanings. Blue offers, green uses, and the colour of
    each kind of file appear in every view, so changing them anywhere shows on
    the screen in front of you. Each meaning has one value for dark grounds and
    one for light, which is your "known values in dark modes and light modes".
    This is where your application-wide instinct holds.

    open beside the map, and its existing choices already apply as you press

  **What an encasing directory hides, as cells (a trial of November 17; [Turn 6
  of November 16](../../AI-Agent-Workspace/ChatHistory/2026/11/2026-11-16.1.reco
  rd.md#turn-6)).** The owner asked whether the "+2 files" of a directory drawn

  Layout → Hidden Files offers three values. Count, the default, is today's
  words. Cells beside name draws, after the directory's own name in the handle's
  line, a closed directory's own small skin for each directory it does not draw
  and its own cell for each such file: one kind of mark at one size, coloured
  as the closed cells are, from the same disclosure as the count. The marks
  stand in the count's room, claiming against the parent's path only the width
  the count's words would take there and running on only into room the whole
  path leaves free; where the line cannot hold the words, the marks take what
  follows the name, as many whole ones as fit, the last veiled where more are
  held. The path is the reader's orientation, and three directories of one
  picture can share a name. Cells under name draws the same marks at the reading
  look as a closed directory's own field, in the 39 units the handle's strip
  already keeps free between the line and the air above the first card. The line
  then holds the chevron, the path, the name and More, with the count's room
  given back to the path. From the connections look out, where the held line
  fills the strip, it is Cells beside name. With either cells value the count's
  words move to the handle's hover text. A press on a cell pins its file, the
  existing pin act, keeping still what a press on the directory's own handle
  keeps still, so the handle and the new card stand in view; unpinning returns
  the cell. A press on a skin opens its directory. Neither press opens or closes
  the handle, and a press on the field's air still does. Each mark is a button,
  "Pin <path>" or "Open <path>" to assistive technology; a cell's hover says
  its file's name alone, a skin's its name and what it holds. A directory's
  marks are one stop in the tab order, moved among by the arrow keys in reading
  order. They are the ground under the wires, at half a closed cell's strength,
  and at full strength with a ring under the pointer or focus. Like every closed
  look they draw over the layout: no box or card moves for them.
