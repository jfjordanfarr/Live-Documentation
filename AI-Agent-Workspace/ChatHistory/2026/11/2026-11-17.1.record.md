### Root's reply to Turn 11, as delivered

**Root, 00:57:27 UTC:**

``````text
**The short version:** the fragility comes from where the expected values live, not from asserting many things at once. I'd split a test into three layers and let each be robust in its own way: the scenario, a captured reading of the page, and judgments over that reading.

## The three layers

1. **Scenario: a map and an address.** The Explorer already puts its state in the link, so a scenario is a link we really opened. On a frozen map (Rosetta, the estate) literal names are safe, because nothing drifts. Tokens are needed in two places: where one scenario runs across the nine Rosetta languages, and on our own growing map, where a token is a description ("the file with the most consumers") resolved at run time.
2. **Reading: what the settled page says, captured as data.** Not pixels and not a DOM dump. For each drawn thing it records what it is, its box, whether its text is whole, its wires and their sides, and its state; plus the address, console errors and network requests. This is your "dataset capture". One instrument produces it for any picture.
3. **Judgments: pure functions over a reading.** They never touch the browser. This is the multi-assertion layer, and each judgment reports separately, so one scenario lists every promise it broke.

## Where robustness comes from: rank every check

| Kind of judgment | What it needs | Example here | Breaks when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Law** (an invariant) | Nothing | Every card inside its directory; no name cut or colliding; uses enter on the left, offerings leave on the right | The promise is broken |
| **Relation** between two readings of one run (testers call these metamorphic) | Nothing | Reload gives the same reading; 2D → 3D → 2D keeps the same files; pin then unpin returns to the start | The promise is broken |
| **Oracle fact** | An independent truth | The wires shown for `{processor}` equal the Rosetta manifest's edges | The product or the fixture's meaning changes |
| **Baseline** (a recorded measurement) | A stored number | Crossings, frames to rest, picture size | Anything moves it |

The rule is to push each check as high up that table as it will go. A literal written into a spec ("328 hidden files in 10 shells") is a baseline pretending to be a law, and that is where most of our fragility sits today.

Baselines still earn their place for things with no law and no oracle, such as how good a layout is. I'd treat them as measurements:
- one-sided bounds ("no more crossings than recorded");
- an explicit re-approval step that shows what moved and by how much.

A deliberate layout change then costs one reviewed update, not forty hand-edited expectations.

## What the split buys beyond speed

- **Judgments can be tested without a browser.** Each one gets a hand-made bad reading that it must reject, which is our rule that a test which cannot fail proves nothing.
- **Readings can be kept.** A new promise can run against yesterday's readings in milliseconds. Kept over time they are also the measurement you mention: today's surprise, that the same search went from about 25 s to about 60 s in a month, would have been a visible curve.
- **Failures read as a table of scenarios against promises.** A whole row red means that scenario cannot load. A whole column red means one promise is broken everywhere, or the check is wrong. A single cell is an edge case.
- **Rosetta gives a relation nobody else has.** The same scenario in nine languages should draw the same shape, within the two documented tiers. That checks the adapters through the UI with no expected data at all.

## Where it can go wrong

- **Laws pass on an empty page.** Each scenario needs a floor: the pinned thing is drawn, and at least one wire exists.
- **The reading instrument can lie.** It must stay small, and a screenshot is saved beside each reading so an eye can confirm that it means what it says.
- **Re-approval can become a rubber stamp.** Diffs must be small and explained.
- **Conversion can silently drop coverage.** I'd keep a ledger mapping each of today's 488 cases to the judgment that replaces it, and for a sample, break the product the way the old case guarded against and confirm the table goes red.
- **Sequences and motion do not fit a single reading.** Journeys become a path of readings with the same laws at every step. Motion is judged from the product's own counters, read at rest. A handful of clock alarms run alone.

## Our own map and slow tests in general

Our own map keeps a seat, but only for laws and relations on a few token-resolved scenarios. Those need no literals, so the repository can grow without breaking them.

The same split applies to the slow unit and integration tests: run the expensive producer once per scenario, keep its output, and judge it many times.

The first slice after the rebuild would be the instrument, about a dozen laws and relations, and Rosetta, measured against the cases it replaces. The gate's rerun is still going; the hand-over follows when it ends.
``````
