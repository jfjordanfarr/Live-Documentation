**Root agent, final response — verbatim; copied during Turn 7:**

````markdown
**Yes. I think recording the exchange as it happens is substantially better than reconstructing it at session end.** It preserves the context behind your words—including what you were correcting or agreeing with—and gives future agents evidence they can revisit after compaction.

I would make it a **session record containing verbatim messages and separately labelled work notes**. That record becomes a source for the linked summaries we’ve just established. This keeps your actual words distinguishable from an agent’s interpretation.

Your beginning-of-turn timing has a particular advantage: the previous response has already been delivered, so we can record what you actually received. I would extend the routine slightly:

- **At the beginning:** append the previous visible response, unless already recorded, and your new prompt. Include steering messages and answers to questions, not just the main prompt.
- **During substantial work:** checkpoint consequential findings, decisions, rejected approaches, and unfinished work. Compaction or interruption can happen before another user turn.
- **Before finishing:** record the outcome, verification, relevant files, and commits. This preserves the work even if there is no next message to trigger capture of the final response.
- **After compaction:** read a short resumption section, then follow links to the particular exchanges needed. Reading the entire accumulated transcript each time would defeat much of the benefit.

The structure needn’t be elaborate. Stable turn headings, labelled speakers, and ordinary Markdown links would give us relationships such as:

> Your request → clarification → decision → changed file → verified commit

That is where this could outperform a compaction summary for recovery: a future agent could recover **why an approach was rejected**, check your exact qualification, or distinguish something proposed from something approved. A compact recap often preserves the conclusion while losing those distinctions.

There are two limits I would make explicit. First, **an agent-maintained record is not automatically a faithful harness export**. If wording is no longer available verbatim, we must mark the gap or label a reconstruction. Second, beginning-only capture leaves the session’s last response vulnerable; recording outcomes before finishing reduces that gap, but doesn’t prove the eventual response was delivered.

The imperative in `AGENTS.md` could stay small, with details in the archive README:

> **Keep the conversation record as you work.** At the beginning of each user turn, preserve the newly received user messages and any preceding visible agent response not yet recorded. Separate verbatim messages from authored interpretation; never reconstruct missing wording as a quotation. Maintain stable turn references, checkpoint consequential work before context is lost, and record outcomes before finishing. After compaction, consult the session’s resumption notes and relevant source turns before continuing. Follow the ChatHistory convention.

I would keep the resumption section short and revisable, while preserving recorded exchanges and adding explicit corrections. That gives us useful working memory without allowing successive agents to quietly rewrite the history they inherited.
````
