# Probe C: systems on a canvas, the estate

_The builder's findings for probe C, verbatim, through four rounds: the round-1 section, the section added when the chain was flipped to provision order, and the round-2, round-3 and round-4 sections written after the owner's verdict. Screenshot numbers refer to the builder's `shots/` folder; kept here are `shots/estate-02-hover-tier2-gateway-hub.png` (02, round 1), `shots/estate-11-provision-canvas-at-rest.png` (11, round 1), `shots/estate-14-provision-gateway-open.png` (14, round 1), `shots/estate-40-canvas-at-rest.png` (40), `shots/estate-42-gateway-open.png` (42), `shots/estate-43-gateway-paymentscontroller.png` (43) and `shots/estate-46-request-path.png` (46)._

## What was built and how to drive it

`build.cjs` reads `.mdmd/index.json`, keeps the 29 files under the estate prefix (27 drawn; the two `expected/*.json` are the oracle's, not a system) and embeds them with `hand-verified-edges.json`, the `projects` array of `compiler-edges.json` and the five `.csproj` files into `index.html`. Nothing else is loaded, so this says nothing about 549 files.

Three scales, one camera; text, pins and dashes keep one pixel size at every scale (CSS2D labels, screen-space sprites, dashes rescaled from camera distance). Boxes are sized by a layered layout of their files, callers left, callees right. Wall pins: one per wire per partner, plus a dangling pin for every `service`, `endpoint` or `connection-string` symbol no wire could use. Tier 1 solid, tier 2 dashed and heavier, tier 3 dotted; every wire is the blue-to-green gradient. Hover fades all but the wire's two boxes and shows the `via` text.

`window.probe`: `level('canvas')`, `open(name)`, `focus(file)`, `out()`, `hover(wireId | 'tunnel' | null)`, `hoverBox(name)`, `orbit(deg)`, `instant(true)`, `state()`. Double-click opens a box or focuses a card; crumbs go out; `shoot.cjs` makes every shot.

## What depth bought

- **The chain stays a line.** Contracts is a library, not a hop. Behind and above the zones (`01`, `07`) its three tier-1 wires arc over the chain, and Portal to Oracle reads uninterrupted.
- **Back-connections are real wires.** A wire that would run right to left or cross a card dips behind the cards, which occlude it (`08`: App.config's wire to the right wall; `09`: portal.js back to Default.aspx). The corset's rule at the folder scale, with no stubs.
- **The card comes to you** (`04`): the focused card lifts toward the camera and unfolds; only its own column slides. Box, other cards, Portal and the tunnel stay put.
- **Declared reads without words**: a dotted cylinder the dashed wire passes through (`01`, `02c`) is plainly not a wire.
- **Transitions keep bearings** (`04` -> `05` -> `06`): the same wall pins, neighbours and crumbs.

## What it cost

- **Perspective breaks the law.** Callee-to-the-right holds only within a plane: Contracts behind PaymentService and 30 units to its right projected to its *left*, so its wire arrived as a back-connection.
- **Occlusion.** HTML labels never occlude: at 30 degrees (`07`) "tunnel · declared" lands on "Hub"; Contracts' barnacle list hangs into SqlServer's label. Symbol rows need baked textures.
- **Scale invariance is work.** World-sized pins were four times bigger at the box scale, dashes likewise; a per-box camera distance made Portal's cards too narrow for their names, so every box is now seen at one px-per-unit and Portal fills the screen.
- **Disorientation.** Opening a box puts the camera inside the zone volume; above 5% face opacity the world turned blue. Elevation drops 21, 9, 4 degrees across scales so cards read frontally, so each transition also tilts.
- **Performance** is not measured by 27 files.

## Facts the picture needed that the docs do not carry

1. **HTTP routes.** The `Gateway/Controllers/PaymentsController.cs` doc has `Post`, `Get`, `Extends: ApiController`; not `[HttpPost] [Route("api/payments")]` nor `[HttpGet] [Route("api/payments/{paymentId}")]`. Wanted at Gateway's left wall (its pin is unlabelled) and at the card scale (`04`), where the Portal wire lands on `Internals` instead of `Post` and `Get`.
2. **The client side of those routes.** The `Portal/Services/GatewayClient.cs` doc lists methods; `"api/payments"`, `"api/payments/" + paymentId` and their base `Portal.GatewayBaseUrl` appear only in summary prose. Wanted at Portal's right wall pin and at the blue pins of `PostPaymentAsync` and `GetPaymentAsync`.
3. **Endpoint addresses.** The docs name `PaymentHub` (kind `endpoint`) in Gateway/Web.config and `Estate.Hub.PaymentHub` (kind `service`) in Hub/App.config, so both ends have labelled pins; `net.tcp://hub.onprem.example:8731/PaymentHub` is in neither, so the wire came from the hand-verified file. Hub/App.config's doc names `PaymentService.Consumer.Production` and `.Staging` without addresses, so the picture cannot say which the wire is: `08` shows an unlabelled wire pin beside two named, dangling pins. The kind pair `endpoint`/`service` gave the direction; the address would let the docs draw the wire.
4. **The connection string's target.** `PaymentsDb` is a `connection-string` symbol; `Server=sql.onprem.example;Database=Payments` is not, so it dangles on PaymentService's right wall while the SqlServer wire uses a separate unlabelled pin.
5. **Procedure and table names.** `PostPaymentProcedure`'s value `dbo.usp_PostPayment`, `[Table("Payment", Schema = "dbo")]` on `Payment`, and `PostPaymentRow`'s result columns exist only as prose or not at all. Wanted at the `PostPayment` row's blue pin, the `Payment` class row and the PaymentService-to-SqlServer wire (`02b`).
6. **Anything in SQL.** No `.sql` file has a Live Doc, so SqlServer and Oracle are ghost boxes whose files are known only as `to` paths of hand-verified edges (`10`), and `FROM ORACLE_CENTRAL..CENTRAL.ACCOUNT` is unobservable. Wanted at both boxes' contents, walls and the SqlServer-to-Oracle wire.
7. **Which files form a system.** Derived from path segments; the truth is `Estate.sln` and the SDK-style `.csproj` globs. Wanted at every box boundary.
8. **Project references.** `<ProjectReference>` (also `compiler-edges.json` `projects[].references`) is the declared tier-1 fact; the picture aggregated 19 file edges instead. Wanted at Contracts' left wall.
9. **Packages and versions.** `EntityFramework 6.5.1`, `Microsoft.AspNet.WebApi.Core 5.3.0`, `System.*` references, `net48`: from `.csproj` only. Wanted at the barnacles.
10. **Zones.** Nothing in the data says cloud or on-prem; the README and a comment in Gateway/Web.config do. Wanted at the two volumes, and for Contracts, which has no zone because it is not deployed, which the data cannot say either.
11. **The tunnel.** Declared on the page. Wanted between the zones.
12. **An edge's tier.** The index has none. `Gateway/Web.config -> Contracts/IPaymentHub.cs` is observed from configuration (`contract=`) but is a plain Dependency, drawn solid. Wanted at every wire's line style.
13. **Evidence.** The hover panel's `via` text is entirely the hand-verified file's.
14. **The symbol a remote edge touches.** Hand-verified edges name files only, so at the card scale every tier-2 wire lands on `Internals`.

## Transition points

- **Canvas to box.** The front face goes to zero, wall pins do not move, wires to other boxes stay, all else fades to 0.32 (`03`). What pulls you in: a closed box's unlabelled wall pins and file count.
- **Box to card.** The card lifts and unfolds; wires leave its rows and still end at the same wall pins (`04`). What pulls you in: folded cards carry row marks that are illegible by design.
- **Out again.** The wire into a wall continues onto the canvas, so the next scale out is visible at the wall.
- **One scale in from the card** (a row's own references) and **one scale out from the canvas** (an estate of snapshots) were not built; the canvas has no place for where a snapshot came from.

## Keep and throw away

Keep: the wall pin as a junction shared by two scales; constant-pixel text, pins and dashes; solid/dashed/dotted for tiers (enough with eight wires; weight added nothing visible); dangling pins; ghost cards and boxes; the evidence panel; barnacle tiles plus a list; zones as volumes.

Throw away: CSS2D for symbol rows; wires dipping behind cards (a hack standing in for a router); height as layout for Contracts; the zone tints if a third hue is unwelcome.

## Open questions

1. The addendum's law (caller left, callee right) is the reverse of the Local Map today, whose left column is "Dependencies (Inputs)" (`layout-math.ts`, class `outbound`). One of them changes.
2. Is Contracts a box or a barnacle? A linked library is cruft on three boxes, not a deployment.
3. Wall pins: one per partner (built), or one per endpoint (what the wall wants, possible only where the docs name a service or endpoint)?
4. Should `expected/*.json` be in the index at all?
5. Is a dangling `PaymentService.Consumer.Staging`, which nothing answers to, signal or noise?

## Which way the chain reads

`probe.direction("provision")`, now the default, and `"call"` lay out the same data two ways; shots `11` to `15` are `01`, `02` and `04` to `06` under provision. Under call the chain reads Portal to Oracle, a wire leaves the caller's blue right wall and lands on the callee's green left wall, callers sit left of callees inside a box, and Contracts hangs behind the far right as the thing everyone calls. Under provision the chain reads Oracle to Portal, a wire leaves the provider's blue right wall and lands on the consumer's green left wall, a file's dependencies sit to its left and its dependents to its right (the Local Map's law), each box's endpoints are on its right wall as the vision's table has them, and Contracts moves behind the far left as the root everything is built on. Nothing else moves: tiers, pins, evidence and the tunnel are the same objects. Provision is the truer picture. It is the owner's sentence read literally: the account balance comes out of Oracle, through the procedure, the entity, the hub and the route, and into the page; what a box offers leaves its right wall and what it needs arrives at its left, so a system reads like a machine with inputs and outputs; and it is the law the file scale already obeys, so a wire means one thing at every scale. Its cost is that the person's click sits at the far right, so a non-software reader has to be told the map runs from the system of record outward, and the hover panel still reads "Gateway → Hub" in the hand-verified file's "from depends on to" convention, against the drawn direction.

## Round 2, 2026-09-28: two dimensions inside, three between

**What changed.** The data model and the direction switch stayed; the rendering was replaced. The world is WebGL with a key light, hemisphere and ambient light, soft shadows on a floor, two zone slabs told apart by value and texture (cloud lighter with a dot grid, on-prem darker with a line grid), fog, and boxes standing above the floor on their cruft: package references as hexagonal nubs on stalks, framework references as barnacle spheres, names on hover, counts at a glance. SqlServer and Oracle are translucent drums with dotted rims. A closed box shows its interior through an 84% front face: the silhouettes of its cards and its wires, unreadable by design. Conduits are 2.6 px solid or 3.2 px dashed with the 10/80/10 gradient; Contracts' three conduits arch over the chain in the vertical plane through their pins. The tunnel is a warm dotted cylinder around the Gateway-Hub conduit, the only warm thing on the canvas. The resting camera stands at the Oracle end, 21 degrees up and 30 degrees off axis, looking along the data's journey toward the Portal; `25` is the same scene 48 degrees round. The interior is a real HTML panel in three-dimensional space (`CSS3DRenderer` on the same camera), sitting at the box's mid-depth plane and revealed by a depth-only plane that lets the world occlude it correctly. Its grammar is the Local Map's: cards with bold name, monospace path, 20 px rows with the kind in small italics, 12 px ring pins on the edges, an `Internals` row, SVG wires with the gradient, dashed for hand-verified edges, corset stubs for back-connections, a count badge where four or more wires meet a pin. Wires from a wall pin to a far column travel a lane above the cards and descend in the column gap; internal wires that skip a column use a lane below. The panel's edges carry the box's wall pins at the same heights as the world's, so a conduit meets the panel where it met the wall. Opening a box dollies the camera square-on to the distance at which one panel pixel is one screen pixel, so text is at its CSS size and never zooms; clicking a card re-renders the panel around it, dependencies left, consumers right, the unrelated cards named in a footnote (`23`). `probe.path(from, to)` draws one request end to end (`26`), lighting the conduits and the interior wires on a shortest dependency path and listing each box's files on it.

**What the critique got right.** All of it that I could test. Flat slabs, hairlines and floating text were debug rendering; lighting, shadow, a floor and thick gradient conduits made a place of the same data in one cycle. The interior belonged in two dimensions: the HTML panel is crisper than any texture and the Local Map's grammar transferred whole. The wall pin as the junction held: the same pin, the same height, in both renderings. Five cycles were needed, and each found something only the picture shows: lanes, projection, occlusion.

**Where I disagree, or cannot comply honestly.** The request path cannot be the owner's sentence yet. The shortest dependency path through Gateway runs `PaymentsController.cs -> GatewaySettings.cs -> Web.config`, not through `HubProxy.cs`, because both are two hops and the docs do not distinguish a call from a read; `26` says "a shortest dependency path" and nothing more. Second, a panel at 1:1 can be wider than the screen: Portal's five columns are 1788 px, so under the no-zoom law the answer is panning, which the page allows. Third, the drums are a guess from the folder name `Database/`; the docs do not say what kind of thing a system is.

**Facts the picture wanted this round and the docs do not carry**, in addition to round 1's fourteen: what kind of system a box is (web app, WCF service, database), wanted at every box's shape; which of two equal-length dependency paths is the call path, wanted at `26`; the count of a project's references, wanted at the cruft (from `.csproj`, as before).

## Round 3, 2026-09-28: the canvas made to read

**What changed.** The chain is the line: conduits are 3.2 px and the three Contracts conduits are 2 px, half-greyed, arched in the vertical plane through their pins with a height that scales to the hop, from a box pushed to the back of the floor; nothing hairpins. The cruft is one cluster of grey nubs on stalks under the front of each underside with its count beside it (`4 references`), names on hover, dimmed in every other state and hidden under an open box. The two floors differ in value, thickness and texture, cloud a lighter dotted slab eight units high, on-prem a darker lined slab five units high, each with a lit edge, and the tunnel bridges the gap between them. Warm is the tunnel's alone. The resting camera stands at the Oracle end 29 degrees up, steep enough that the far floor reaches the top of the frame; every system is in the frame, and the orbit shot dollies out a little so Portal stays whole. Interiors fade with their boxes, faster than the face they are seen through, so a dimmed box shows less of its guts than one at rest, and on the request path nothing is lit above its resting value: the path's boxes keep their cards and wires at rest while everything else fades. Inside a panel the wall's wires are the tails and heads of the conduits outside, green all the way in and blue all the way out, and a wire crosses an empty column straight; only a card in the way sends it round by a lane. At the file scale, files in other boxes wired to the focused one appear as compact stand-in cards at the end of their wall wire, named with their box and marked "beyond the wall", so an empty CONSUMERS column now holds Portal's `GatewayClient.cs` (`33`); the panel grows about its centre when a column needs more room, so the focused card and the wall pins do not move. Off-frame neighbours are named at the frame's edge where their conduits leave it. Labels never overlap: box names step up from one another, other labels step away, and none leaves the frame. The evidence card sits above the conduit and is titled in the reading's direction ("Hub serves Gateway"). One chrome block, bottom left, four lines, with the status folded in; one instruction line. On return the just-visited box keeps a faint outline (`34`).

**What the critique got right.** The eye did land on the arcs; the fix was not to remove them but to recede them, and the chain became the line at once. Dimming a face without dimming what is behind it lit the wrong thing. The stand-in card is the right answer to an empty column: it names the far end of the wall's wire and gives the viewer the next box to open. The trace costs nothing and answers "where was I".

**Where I disagree, or cannot comply.** Eighty per cent of the width and every system in frame cannot both hold at 16:9 for a chain of seven; the frame now uses the whole width with nothing cut, and the empty top was the sky, cured by the steeper camera rather than a closer one, which cut the near end in every trial. Hub cannot be in frame while Gateway's panel is at one pixel per pixel: it is 1140 px beyond the panel's edge; the edge tab is the honest substitute, and Portal's wall pin does reach the frame. The request path is still a shortest dependency path, for the reason given in round 2.

**Facts the picture wanted this round and the docs do not carry.** None new. The stand-in cards want the same facts as round 1's first five items: what the wire between two files carries (a route, a contract, a table), which the docs do not say, so a stand-in can only name the file and, where the docs have it, the symbol.

## Round 4, 2026-09-28: the last pass before the owner

**What changed.** The wall pins own a band at the panel's mid-height that no card may occupy: a column's cards stack below and above it in turn, a focused card straddles it, and a wall wire now runs straight at pin height and bends only into its column, so the Contracts wire is a bus with branches instead of a border along the ceiling (`42`). Four or more wires into one pin meet at a junction and one trunk carries them the last 52 px, with the count on the junction, so the badge replaces the fan (`43`). Pin labels sit above their pins, alternating below for the next pin so none lies on a neighbour's wire, and a pin whose wire reaches nothing inside is dimmed. The panel carries its own title on its corner and the world's name label yields to it; when a grown panel reaches the top chrome the title yields too. Cards are 276 px so a stand-in's path fits. The resting camera backed off a step at the same elevation with the chain climbed into the upper band, so every body is whole (`40`); the second view is the mirror of the first, from the Portal end, because a frontal view cannot fill the middle of the frame and keep Portal (`45`). The evidence card sits above and left of the conduit it explains, its file pair written in the drawing's direction, and its provenance line says the hand-verified file writes the consumer first (`41`). The path sentence takes the instruction line's place while a path is drawn; each box's list is wrapped to the box's width and ordered as the wires draw (`46`). Counts hang centred under their clusters and vanish on boxes narrower than 150 px; tabs align with where a conduit crosses the frame; ghost rims are faint and solid so dotted is the tunnel's alone; the trace is half as bright; the cloud slab is a value step lighter with a dot grid that reads from the far end. Labels never rise into the top chrome.

**What the critique got right.** The ceiling lane was the panel's brightest line and said nothing; a bus at pin height says "from the wall, into these rows". A fan beside a count was two statements of one fact. An evidence card on the far box's name hid the thing it named. Wrapped, direction-ordered lists made the path shot readable at a glance.

**Where I disagree, or cannot comply.** Filling the middle two thirds from the front and keeping Portal cannot both hold; the second view is oblique from the other end instead, and the chain reads there as one line with two lit floors and the gap under the tunnel. From that angle Hub's name crosses its own pins; the label pass moves labels off labels, not off pins, and a pin-aware pass is the next thing to build. The file scale still grows the panel rather than moving the camera; when it reaches the chrome the title hides, which is the smaller loss.

**Facts the picture wanted this round and the docs do not carry.** None new; the junction count, the stand-ins and the wall runs all draw facts the index and the hand-verified file already hold.
