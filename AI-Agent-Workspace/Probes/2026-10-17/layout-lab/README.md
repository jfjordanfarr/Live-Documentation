# The 3D picture's drift, and the restoring force that ends it

_A layout-lab measurement of October 17, 2026, by the root Claude Code agent (Fable 5.1), for ticket C5 of [the change-over-time plan](../../../../.mdmd/layer-3/change-over-time.mdmd.md#open-questions). The owner's observation that opened it ([Turn 3 of the day's record](../../../ChatHistory/2026/10/2026-10-17.1.record.md#turn-3)): "just enable and disable certain archetypes/file types over and over and see the microgalaxies drift further apart. What this tells me is that whatever simulation drives tension in the 3D force graph is highly lax"; and their direction ([Turn 5](../../../ChatHistory/2026/10/2026-10-17.1.record.md#turn-5)) on "a weak gravity, toward the origin or toward a sphere's parent hub": "Boom. Exactly." The reports beside this file are the lab's; the numbers in them are historical measurements, not a runtime guarantee._

## Brief

Reproduce the drift headlessly over the page's own forces and data, name its cause, add the restoring force as a lever beside a bound on the repulsion's reach, and choose their defaults by two measures: a file type toggled out and back five times must leave the picture's extent where it began, and the spheres that never left must move least on each restore. Picture the chosen setting against today's in the built Explorer for the owner's eye.

## What the simulation was

Four forces of D3's, read from `forceGraphLayout.ts` and the library's source (d3-force-3d 3.0.6; three-forcegraph's default forces are link, charge and centre): a spring on each pulling link toward its preferred length; the repulsion of every sphere on every other, with no bound on its reach; a collision force that keeps spheres apart and ignores alpha; and a centring force that only translates the whole picture so that its mean sits at the origin. Between two clusters that share no link there is repulsion alone, and nothing pulls the whole inward; so a reheat (which every membership change is, at full alpha) pushes the clusters a little further apart, and the centring keeps their midpoint in place while the picture grows. The restoring force is a gravity, D3's radial force at radius zero: a pull on every sphere toward the origin in proportion to its distance, so that the repulsion has something to balance against and the picture has an equilibrium it returns to.

## Method

`scripts/layout-lab/drift.ts`: settle the full picture (the cold start's filters with the toggled type shown), remove one file type and re-settle, restore it with every sphere where it last stood and a hub new to the picture at its members' centroid, re-settle; five cycles, each settle three hundred ticks at full alpha, which is the page's update path today. Per cycle: the root-mean-square distance of every sphere from the centroid (the extent), the box's diagonal, and the mean and greatest move of the spheres that never left. Swept over this repository's bundle (723 files) and the estate's, in both layouts, at gravity 0, 0.02, 0.05 and 0.1 with unbounded reach, then reach 200 and 500 at gravity 0 and 0.05. The toy case is `drift.test.ts`: two clusters that never reference each other.

## What it found

**A reheat with nothing changed expands the picture.** The first sweep ran the control by accident: its `--toggle tests` matched no archetype (the docs say `test`), so each cycle reheated the unchanged picture twice from its carried positions, and its own table said so, "Spheres (toggled) 780 (0)". The verb now maps the option to the archetype, refuses any other word, and names the control `--toggle none`; the measure throws when a toggle names files the picture does not hold. What the accident measured stands, and is the owner's drift in its purest form: under the forces as they were, this repository's connections picture grew from an extent of 890 to 2,434 over five reheats (2.73 times) with the unchanged spheres moving a mean of 336 units per reheat, its structure layout from 969 to 1,949 (2.01 times), and the estate's connections from 208 to 465 (2.24 times); the estate's structure layout, one tree of 53 spheres, held within 5 percent. With gravity the picture settled smaller and stayed where it settled: on this repository's connections layout, gravity 0.02 held the extent within 8 percent over five reheats, 0.05 within 3 and 0.1 within 2, the settled extents 580, 415 and 317 against 890, and the spheres' moves per reheat 93, 63 and 47 units against 336; the structure layout the same within 10, 5 and 2 percent. A bound on the repulsion's reach alone (200 or 500 units) slowed the growth to 1.41 times over five reheats and did not stop it, since nothing yet pulled the clusters back; with gravity 0.05 the reach made the settled picture smaller again (223 at 200, 343 at 500, 415 unbounded) and the moves smaller (31, 56, 63), the extent within 1 percent in every case. The tables of that run were deleted as mislabeled and are reproduced by the `--toggle none` rows of the rerun.

**The toggle itself, 199 test files out and back in** ([repository-drift-tests-gravity.md](repository-drift-tests-gravity.md); the control is [repository-drift-none-gravity.md](repository-drift-none-gravity.md), with the same numbers as the accident's). Under today's forces the repository's connections picture grew to 2.55 times its settled extent over five cycles (890 to 2,273) with the unchanged spheres moving a mean of 324 units per restore and the farthest 1,153; the structure layout, 213 of its 1,061 spheres toggled, to 1.87 times (969 to 1,814) with a mean move of 290. Gravity 0.02 held the connections picture within 7 percent (580 to 619) and the structure layout within 9 (665 to 728), the moves 84 and 117; 0.05 within 3 and 5 percent (415 to 427; 488 to 512), the moves 64 and 79; 0.1 within 2 and 3 percent, the moves 51 and 53, at a settled extent 36 percent of today's. The estate's bundle holds no test file, so its rows ([estate-drift-tests-gravity.md](estate-drift-tests-gravity.md)) are a second control: its connections picture of 35 spheres grew 2.24 times under today's forces and held within 10, 6 and 4 percent at the three gravities; its structure layout, one small tree, barely moved at any setting. The reach alone ([repository-drift-tests-reach.md](repository-drift-tests-reach.md)): 200 or 500 units without gravity slowed the growth to 1.24 to 1.42 times and did not stop it; with gravity 0.05 the reach made the settled picture smaller again (223 at 200, 343 at 500) and the moves smaller (32, 61), the extent within 2 percent either way.

**Three synthetic shapes** ([synthetic-drift.md](synthetic-drift.md)), a toy of two clusters, a mid-sized one of four and a large one of eight, with no link between clusters: the connections layout drifted in every size without gravity (3.17, 2.73 and 3.07 times); gravity 0.02 held the mid shape (1.04) but not the toy (1.18) or the large (1.16); 0.05 held every shape within 7 percent; 0.1 within 5 at about 40 percent of the ungoverned extent. A synthetic tree is one component through its root hub and did not drift in the structure layout; this repository's real tree did (1.87 times), its size letting a full reheat spread it.

## What was chosen

Gravity 0.05 and the reach left unbounded, as the shipped defaults: the smallest force that held every measured shape, the repository's two layouts, the estate's, and the three synthetic ones, within a tenth over five changes, with the unchanged spheres' move on a change cut to a fifth of today's (64 against 324 on the repository's connections layout). What it costs is spread: the settled picture is about half of today's extent (415 against 890). Gravity 0.02 keeps two thirds of today's spread but lets the toy and the large synthetic shape grow by a sixth; 0.1 is stiller again at a third of the spread. The reach is left unbounded because, by these measures, it buys little once gravity exists and costs spread; it stays a lever. These are starting defaults from the lab's reading; the owner's eye decides from the pictures in [Screenshots/2026-10-17](../../../Screenshots/2026-10-17/README.md), where ten toggles are shown before and after under today's forces and under gravity 0.02, 0.05 and 0.1, and the sliders under Tuning let them turn either lever by hand. Kept by `forceGraphLayout.test.ts`, which holds the shipped tuning to a compact equilibrium on the toy (within a tenth over five cycles; it failed at 3.16 times under the forces as they were and at 1.19 under 0.02), and by `drift.test.ts`, which holds the measure itself.
