# T3 3D responsiveness — October 29, 2026

Historical diagnostic and repair record. Triggered by [the owner's failed interactive trial](../../../ChatHistory/2026/10/2026-10-29.1.record.md#turn-3) of the October 28 T3 bundle. The bounded rendering repair passed the full gate; the broader control-system design and large-scale settling remain open.

## The first repair did not resolve usability

After trying the fresh client, the owner again reported a halt and then clarified that camera/sidebar/search technically respond but have terrible response times ([Turns 8–9](../../../ChatHistory/2026/10/2026-10-29.1.record.md#turn-8)). [Sustained-progress observations](sustained-progress.json) separate engine calls, actual position revisions, draws and completed physics ticks. With all 27,823 nodes and 80,108 links present, accepted position changes arrived roughly every ten seconds. An 80-second interval completed one outer tick; 670 physics slices occupied 6.950 seconds. There were no runtime errors or external physics refreshes in that interval. The physics advances, but much of the time between its small slices is spent repeatedly preparing unchanged scene objects, testing native pointer intersections and recalculating camera framing. Passing earlier browser tests and counting animation callbacks did not establish sustained usability.

[Direct CPU/GPU timing](draw-cost.json) narrows the rendering diagnosis: five actual draws took 7.432–7.796 ms on the RTX 3090 GPU, in two draw calls with about 7.75–7.82 million triangles. JavaScript preparation/submission took 236.0–269.8 ms, of which batch preparation took 201.1–235.1 ms. GPU queries were not disjoint. These are observations of the current camera and unfolding state, not a claim of uniform performance at every zoom or a guaranteed speedup from a future renderer.

The root proposed replacing the per-node/per-wire native scene-object layer with direct batches and separating layout progress from repainting. The owner approved trying it in [Turn 10](../../../ChatHistory/2026/10/2026-10-29.1.record.md#turn-10). That candidate must carry over camera movement, picking, dragging, labels, filters and history; preserve the physical model and graph; and be evaluated through sustained interaction and movement on T3 and the smaller repositories. No new force default or adaptive tuning policy is selected.

The preserved bundle is served through the existing Explorer task on port 8900. The root entered its Connections/3D view with Show Tests enabled and no pins, before receiving the owner's exact four-pin URL. This is a larger workload than yesterday's tests-hidden measurement. It reproduces severe stuttering, not yet the exact reported freeze.

The integrated Chromium browser reports hardware acceleration through ANGLE/D3D11 on an NVIDIA RTX 3090, at 1065×622 and device pixel ratio 1. These measurements are not SwiftShader results. [The two CPU-profile summaries](browser-profile.json) retain self-times, sampled functions, main-thread long tasks and the environment reading. They are summaries of Chrome's program profiler, not agent traces.

- A 30.899-second sample observed animation gaps up to 1,683.5 ms and repeated main-thread tasks around 650–770 ms. The timing observation began before this sample, so those maxima describe the observed opening/settling session rather than only this profile window.
- A 21.517-second sample puts 3,728 ms of self-time in the per-mesh callback of `drawSpatialGraphOnChange`; garbage collection accounts for 1,274 ms. Scene traversal, matrix composition, native link updates, batching and projected names occupy much of the remainder. The physics tree walk is a smaller cost.
- The served bundle's zero-based line 5987, column 6159 corresponds to the draw guard's fresh per-mesh snapshot arrays and Map. Line 4943, column 25041 is the library's link-position update; line 6026, column 36893 is name projection. These were mapped back to the checked-in source rather than guessed from minified names.

The 10 ms setting bounds one portion of the physics work; it is not a bound on the complete animation frame. The page still repeatedly traverses and updates the native scene, and the draw guard builds a complete fresh snapshot even when a paced physics part has not yet moved any positions. Determining which of those passes can be removed without changing the picture or interactions is the next diagnostic step.

The initial profiler helper failed because its host lacks a global timer; the two successful samples use Playwright's wait. No failed helper is counted as product evidence. The owner's precise freeze symptoms and URL were requested asynchronously and remain pending at this checkpoint.

## Candidate observations

[Frame observations](frame-observations.json) preserve the subsequent samples. The baseline and first candidate used the original visual settings; the later candidate uses wire resistance 0.2 after the owner's density finding. These sample different moments of settling and framing, so they are indicative workload observations, not a matched-state speedup benchmark.

- Baseline, 27,823 nodes / 80,108 links: 24 animation frames in 9,467 ms while moving; after explicitly stopping physics, 37 in 9,970 ms. A stationary scene was still expensive.
- First candidate: 84 frames in 9,962 ms while moving; 364 in 10,007 ms after stopping physics. Every one of 100 sampled positions moved during the live interval.
- A following first-candidate trace measured 107 native engine calls taking 5,404 ms, but just one actual WebGL pass, 289 ms, with 255 ms in batch preparation. This motivated retaining batch classification and removing the inert link force.
- Latest candidate, physics running: 120 frames in 10,261 ms, median gap 83.3 ms, p95 100.1 ms, maximum 383.3 ms. All sampled positions changed and the full graph remains. This is not a 60 fps claim.

The code changes preserve the force settings and membership: actual position/appearance revisions govern redraw, labels and picking; unchanged wire endpoints retain their transforms; batch classification and working lists are reused; link endpoints bind on membership changes rather than through a zero-strength spring force every frame; camera bounds avoid per-sphere vector allocations. The full gate passed 1,452 unit, 113 integration and 208 browser tests, with one worker and no browser retries. No timing limit or correctness assertion was relaxed. Manual inspection, pinning and recentering worked on the full T3 scene; a reader overlay intercepting a Pin attempt and a later stale hover target were recorded as unsuccessful attempts rather than counted as passes.

## Direct renderer trial

The owner's [Turn 10](../../../ChatHistory/2026/10/2026-10-29.1.record.md#turn-10) authorized replacing the scene-object round trip with direct instance buffers. Three geometry meshes and two lights now carry the full graph; physical motion, camera gestures, picking, dragging, attention, names and history retain their roles. This removes the native per-object scene-update and interaction layer. It does not select a worker, an interaction cutoff, reduced graph membership or new visual defaults.

The [first direct profile](direct-first-profile.json) exposed repeated framing and name work, which was reduced before the [four-minute trial](direct-sustained.json): all 27,823 nodes / 80,108 links, 104 actual changes in sampled uploaded positions, no errors, changed-frame median 189.5 ms and p95 313.3 ms; GPU median 6.53 ms. The direct WebGL submission call's 1.5 ms median omits buffer preparation and must not be quoted as the whole drawing cost. The owner independently reported increasing navigability but continued slowness in [Turns 11–12](../../../ChatHistory/2026/10/2026-10-29.1.record.md#turn-11).

Further preparation reductions share projected endpoints among incident wires, write typed attributes directly, retain the preceding depth order as the next exact sort's input, and reuse projection working vectors for names. A [three-minute trial](direct-preparation-sustained.json) retained the full graph, recorded 124 actual position changes and no errors; changed-frame median was 164.4 ms, p95 199.1 ms and maximum 251.1 ms. That trial includes profiler and camera/hover activity, and samples a different unfolding moment from the earlier run: these are workload observations, not a matched-state speedup claim. Physics was never deliberately paused in either finite trial.

The [camera profile](direct-preparation-camera.json), 20.074 seconds and 101 draws, still puts preparation and sorting first. The separate [dense-hover profile](direct-preparation-hover.json), 5.632 seconds, samples 249.848 ms in wire picking and 66.069 ms in node picking, about 5.6% together. The octree walk accounts for 1,941.497 ms, about 34.5%, while the camera is still. These are self-times: hover-triggered attention/appearance work is separate. The owner's question about subpixel interaction is useful but removing these hit tests would leave most measured work. No cutoff is selected. An index of projected candidates could narrow picking while preserving every target; moving camera-dependent appearance into GPU work is a further rendering direction to evaluate, with exact transparency order and picking correspondence still to preserve.

A matched-data camera investigation is in [the zoom comparison](zoom-comparison.json): the retired controls measured the initial 300×150 canvas and missed its resize to 1339×950. The direct controls measure the real canvas. The close-range browser test now uses a bounded number of real gestures to reach its unchanged near-limit prerequisite, instead of relying on excessive sensitivity from the wrong size. A resize-only history step also exposed a stale sphere radius: symbol counts now participate in the physical field's input signature, and a data render refreshes cached appearance. The regression checks uploaded radius, unchanged positions at zero update energy and retained wire bindings.

The first direct full gate was deliberately interrupted for this additional work after 1,452 unit, 113 integration and 146 browser passes, one browser case interrupted and 61 not run. It was not a full pass. A second gate was stopped after 36 browser passes for the measured packed-array improvement below. The packed build’s complete gate passed: 1,453 unit, 113 integration and 209 browser tests, with one browser worker and no retries (31.1 minutes). Manual checks cover both this workspace and the estate sample. T3's frame-rate and readability problems remain open regardless of their eventual result.

### Packed numeric preparation

A [same-state scratch gather](direct-gather-comparison.json) produced identical endpoint matrices while reading packed numeric positions much faster than repeated graph-object fields. The renderer now reads each mutable node position once per draw, binds wires to numeric endpoint indices at membership changes, and uses numeric appearance, bounds and depth arrays through the hot loops. Stable datum bindings remain for picking. Depth stays double precision and exact ties retain creation order; the unit regression permutes membership to check endpoint rebinding and distinguishes depths that collapse together in Float32.

The completed [packed trial](direct-packed-sustained.json) stayed visible for three minutes: all 27,823 nodes / 80,108 wires, five scene objects, 629 draws, 158 sampled uploaded-position changes and no captured errors. Changed-frame CPU median was 69.1 ms, p95 88.5 ms and maximum 131.2 ms; idle median 13 ms. The initial packed load in a hidden editor tab was discarded, not treated as a freeze or measured performance. None of the successful finite trials deliberately stopped physics.

The [packed camera profile](direct-packed-camera.json) recorded 137 draws over 11.846 seconds. Exact sorting, buffer preparation, name placement and physics remain costs. The separate [hover profile](direct-packed-hover.json), 4.720 seconds, samples 45.524 ms in node picking and 36.193 ms in wire picking (1.7% together), and 2,158.872 ms in the octree walk (45.7%). Work has shifted toward physics while the camera is stationary; camera movement still needs preparation/sorting. These samples differ in simulation state and include profiling overhead, so their comparisons are indicative rather than matched-state guarantees. Keeping all targets interactive has not prevented this improvement.

The full graph first appears by 8.20 seconds in the packed trial (10.55 seconds in the first direct trial); frame statistics start after 20 seconds, beyond reveal in every successful sample. Packed accepted-position gaps still have median 1,398.7 ms and p95 1,785.1 ms. Drawing can respond to camera input between these updates. Smoother camera response and more frequent physical progress are separate outcomes; this work improves both but does not make settling run at the drawing frame rate.
