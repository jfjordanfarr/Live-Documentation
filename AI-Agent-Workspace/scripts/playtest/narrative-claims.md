# Narrative playtest: the grading key

Current as of 2026-11-19. This is the grading key for the narrative playtest: a list of dated claims about how Live Documentation evolved, drawn from [the narrative](../../Memory/narrative.md) and written before any tester ran, so that nothing in it is fitted to an answer. It is never shown to a tester. A grader compares the tester's account, made only from the Explorer's map and its History scrubber, against these claims. The Explorer's history covers the first commit on 2025-10-16 to commit `5d520791` on 2026-11-13 and draws no file under `AI-Agent-Workspace/`; it also draws no generated or authored markdown documents, so the `.mdmd/` tree, the READMEs and AGENTS.md never appear on the map. "Showable" means a person watching files and directories outside `AI-Agent-Workspace/` appear, change, move or vanish, with the scrubber's dates, could see the claim; "picture" means file and directory names, counts and changes are enough, and "words" means the claim can only be read from a commit subject in the step panel. Dates are the commit dates the history shows. "History" in the last column means the claim was checked by replaying `dist/explorer/explorer-history.json`; "git" means read-only git commands.

**Counts.** 27 showable against 12 not showable; of the showable, 23 picture against 4 words.

**What the history cannot carry.** The scrubber shows where files came and went, and by inference what the software was made of at each date, but nothing of why. It cannot show who or what made a change: the project's turns from Copilot to Gemini to Claude Code and Codex, or the owner's part as the eye and the agents' as builders, leave no file. It cannot show decisions that changed meaning before they changed code, such as the simplification of November 2025 or the choice of history as the next big thing. It cannot show the failures that shaped the rules: lost uncommitted work, tests reported green before they finished, thresholds lowered and benchmarks that graded themselves (the files of a self-grading benchmark are visible, the self-grading is not). It cannot show the owner's reasons, their aphantasia, their burnout, their judgments of pictures, or anything said in the chats. And because the map draws no markdown, the documentation that is the product, the vision and AGENTS.md change invisibly: a commit that rewrote the project's steering looks the same as a commit that touched only the record.
