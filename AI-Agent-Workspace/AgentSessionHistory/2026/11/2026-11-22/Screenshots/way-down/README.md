## The trial

**Historical evidence, 2026-11-22.** Taken by the trial's builder between 01:55 and 02:26 UTC on its branch's built site (the estate's pictures by 02:13, before two last changes that touch only a thing with more files than a picture holds; this repository's after them), 1,280 by 800, headless Chromium through the browser lane, each state in a fresh page with the continuing search off and Tuning → 2D Layout → **Way Down** set as named: *Folder Alone*, the default, or *Carries The Question*. The trial is described in [the Explorer design](../../../../../../../.mdmd/layer-3/live-documentation-explorer.mdmd.md#independent-exploration), "The way down from the World Map, a trial". An arrival was taken 150 ms after the press, while the views cross-fade and the camera eases, and again at rest. The builder looked at every picture; the eye that decides is the owner's.

**What a picture holds.** A way down pins files while the cards they bring, themselves and the files a line joins to them, stay within two dozen, one file for each wire before a second for any. Three rules were tried on `engine` and looked at, one after another. Counted in files, six: 162 cards, and `explorer` 200. Counted in cards with the first file pinned whatever it brought: one file of forty-six and 43 cards, since its first file alone overran the budget and left room for no other. Passing over such a file for the next that fits, pinned alone only where nothing fits: two files and 26 cards, the pictures above. On the second of those the counting line, drawn after the camera was placed, pushed the map down and engine's label under itself; it now stands before the map is drawn.

**What the design audit found in these arrivals** (run at 03:36 UTC over hub, portal and the wire opened under the trial). Nothing collides. Row names are cut: the arrivals rest zoomed out, at the look where a card's rows stand in fixed slots, and a long row name is cut there to its slot with an ellipsis; five rows in the hub's arrival, seven in the portal's, eight in the wire's, the longest "net.tcp://payments.onprem.example:8732/PaymentService", by 210 px. So "every name reads" above is true of files and folders and not of rows: the row that names the hub's address reads "net.tcp://hub.onprem. example:8731/…". At reading size, where Folder Alone arrives, no row is cut. It is the look's own rule and the price of framing a thing with what its lines reach; the browser cases hold that nothing collides and that nothing but a row's name is cut.

### A path told as asked

**Historical evidence, 2026-11-22, 03:33 UTC.** Not part of the trial: a default, for every path the page answers, by the owner's word of that day ("Let's go with your recommendation on it", Turn 5 of the day's record), the recommendation being that a route asked page first is told page first. Taken by the same builder on its branch's built site, 1,280 by 800, the lever at its default. The drawing is unchanged; what changed is that the question is answered and how it is told. It is described in [the Explorer design](../../../../../../../.mdmd/layer-3/live-documentation-explorer.mdmd.md#pathfinding-rendering).

The builder's eye prefers the first, on the evidence of November 21: testers hedged every direction they read from a mark and were right every time they read it in words. The wording is the owner's to settle.

**The arrival of a path asked against the drawing.** A path's own arrival rests on its first file at the left, which for such a path is the end asked for, with the file asked from off to the right, perhaps far. So it is framed instead on the file asked from with the file it uses beside it, by the frame the trial's ways down use. That frame is a card's, at a card's reading size, and so stands larger than a path's usual arrival; the two directions of one route therefore arrive at two sizes. The path's own fit, resting on its last file where the asking ran that way, would be the same size both ways and was not touched here, the arrival's camera being another builder's that day.
