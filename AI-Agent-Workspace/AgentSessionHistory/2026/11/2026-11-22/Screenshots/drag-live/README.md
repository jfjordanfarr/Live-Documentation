**Historical evidence, 2026-11-22.** Taken by the builder on its own builds of this repository's map, 1,440 by 900, in the Connections layout with `packages/engine/src/live-docs/graph.ts` pinned, the repository's most connected file. A temporary instrument waited for rest, took the pinned sphere, carried it 280 px to the right and 120 px up in forty moves of the pointer with 20 ms between them, read every sphere's place with the button still down, held two seconds more, let go and waited for rest. It ran on the container's graphics card, and once on the slowed machine of [the weak-machine probe](../../../2026-11-21/Probes/weak-machine/README.md) (WebGL in software, the page's processor slowed four times).

## Why

The owner, 2026-11-22: "I notice that the 3D graph stops simulating rather quickly. It's efficient, which is awesome, but the one place I would want that responsiveness back is when I'm clicking and dragging an item and swinging the graph around with me."

## What was wrong

The forces did run while a sphere was held; the picture is never said to be at rest with a sphere in a hand. But they ran at a crawl, for two reasons, both in how the motion is paced against frames:

1. A dragged sphere is moved from outside at every frame. A frame after such a move was held to one evaluation of the field, a rule made so that a newly entered piece of a picture is drawn first where it was placed. Under a moving hand that was every frame, and the one evaluation was often only the reading of the forces again.
2. A frame took at most one tick of the motion. This picture, 183 spheres and 12 directory shells with their files drawn, gave a frame every 64 to 88 ms during the drag on the graphics card, at the median, and every 290 ms or so on the slowed machine, so a tick a frame is a fraction of the pace a settle has at sixty frames a second.

[The picture before](before--card--hand-arrived.png) shows it: the pinned file at the upper right where the hand has carried it, and a fan of its wires stretched back to the files tied to it, still where they stood.

## What the builder's eye sees

The two pictures of the hand's arrival are not as different as the numbers. Before, the pinned file stands at the upper right with a fan of wires stretched back across a third of the picture to files that have barely stirred. After, the two large shells have come with it and the fan is thinner, but it is still a fan: most tied files are a little past half of the way they will go, and the way they will go is itself only half the hand's.

So the mends make the forces live under a hand, about twice as quick to answer, and they do not yet give "swinging the graph around with me". What stands between is no longer the pacing. It is the tether, which holds every sphere toward where it stood when the drag began, as it does through every change so that a person keeps their bearings, and frames of some 68 ms in a picture this full. A graph that swings with the hand would need the tether to let go of what is tied to the held sphere for as long as it is held, or to take hold again where the hand has carried things. That changes where a picture rests after a drag, which the brief asked to keep, so it was not built; it is the fork this folder leaves.

## What follows and what does not

- **Files tied to the dragged file** follow, and with them the directory shells its group's hidden files stand in, which came further than the spheres at every stage.
- **Everything else in the picture moves a little too.** All 183 spheres moved by more than a unit, the seeds of groups the pin does not reach and the lone files among them, which no wire ties to the hand; which of the forces moves them was not looked into.
- **A tied file never goes the hand's whole way.** The tether holds every sphere toward where it stood when the drag began, so in the end the spheres had gone about half as far as the hand (103 units of its 217 to 224).
- **Not looked at:** a wrapped group taken by its seed, and a veiled body, under the hand; and how the slowed machine follows with both mends, which was measured with the first alone.
