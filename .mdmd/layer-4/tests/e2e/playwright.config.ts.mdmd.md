## Authored
### Purpose

Playwright configuration for the Explorer's browser suite: one Chromium worker over the pre-built static bundle when Playwright is run directly, and the cases and workers of each of the gate's two passes, the web server that serves it, the reporters, the page's seeded settings, and how the browser is launched.

### Notes

- Created in [Dev Day 85](../../../../AI-Agent-Workspace/AgentSessionHistory/2026/03/2026-03-30/2026-03-30.1.md) as part of the Playwright E2E infrastructure setup.
- Runs a single Chromium worker against a pre-built static explorer bundle served via `http-server` on port 8766.
- HTML reports are written to `reports/e2e/` (gitignored); screenshots and traces are captured only on failure.
- One worker, and the files' tests in order, by the owner's rule of 2026-10-19: "E2E playwright must be serialized. Can't be parallellized unless we want to enjoy flaky test fails" ([Turn 12 of the October 17 session](../../../../AI-Agent-Workspace/AgentSessionHistory/2026/10/2026-10-17/2026-10-17.1.record.md#turn-12)). This note had justified it by a shared browser context and port, neither of which held; the measured reason ([Turn 11](../../../../AI-Agent-Workspace/AgentSessionHistory/2026/10/2026-10-17/2026-10-17.1.record.md#turn-11)) is the box: a 3D test's software renderer (SwiftShader in the headless shell, a 26-thread GPU process) takes about nine of the ten cores alone, so under three workers each test ran about one and a half times as long as alone and under four about twice, with 7 and then 9 of the 145 tests over their budgets by three to six seconds, none failing on a fact, and four 2D tests that wait on timed behaviour already take 25 to 29 of their 30 seconds alone. The suite took 9.7 minutes on three workers and 8.7 on four, against 20 on one; the design that would have made more workers honest (3D tests taking turns, budgets that fit the waiting tests) was declined as too complex.
- Re-measured on 2026-11-02, the rule unchanged, on this box's ten cores of two hardware threads each (twenty to `nproc`): against 34.7 minutes on one worker, two workers took 23.6 with 3 of the 265 tests failing and three took 20.9 with 8 failing, and the 2D tests alone, with WebGL off, failed 4 of 156 on four workers; every failure was a test near its budget or one sampling a picture in motion, and each passed on one worker. A 3D test's renderer keeps about fourteen of the twenty threads busy alone. The measurements, and two launch settings that shorten settles without changing the settled picture, are in [the probe](../../../../AI-Agent-Workspace/AgentSessionHistory/2026/11/2026-11-02/Probes/e2e-speed/README.md).
- A `storageState` seeds every test's page with the Local Map's continuing search off (`searchStarts: 0`), so that a picture read after it is drawn is the picture it first drew; a spec that seeds the tuning itself chooses, and the search has its own spec (2026-10-07).

- Since [2026-11-05](../../../../AI-Agent-Workspace/AgentSessionHistory/2026/11/2026-11-05/2026-11-05.1.record.md#turn-8) a trace is kept only for a failure and without DOM snapshots. Recording a full trace for every test and discarding it on a pass took about a sixth of a 36-test subset's time (5.7 minutes as configured, 5.2 with the snapshots left out, 4.8 with the trace off; one worker, the same bundles), and one failing test's trace held 49 snapshots totalling 57 MB, two of them 29 MB, written to the 9p mount; the action log, console, network and screencast remain for a failure. The same day's measurements, in [the gate-time probe](../../../../AI-Agent-Workspace/AgentSessionHistory/2026/11/2026-11-05/Probes/gate-time/README.md), found pinning the browser to one thread per core gaining nothing on that subset, so it is not adopted, and the owner's one-worker rule stands.
- Since 2026-11-17 the browser is launched by `browser-launch.ts`, on the host's graphics card where the container has one, and a global setup, `renderer-report.ts`, prints the run's WebGL renderer first ([Turn 1 of the November 17 session](../../../../AI-Agent-Workspace/AgentSessionHistory/2026/11/2026-11-17/2026-11-17.1.record.md#turn-1)). On the card the eleven cases that were red or on their time limits at the close of November 16 ran in 5.8 minutes and ten passed, the two the owner keeps red on their clocks among them ([the measurements](../../../../AI-Agent-Workspace/AgentSessionHistory/2026/11/2026-11-17/2026-11-17.1.record.md#work-notes-turn-2)). The whole suite then ran on the card at one worker: 482 of 488 in 98 minutes, none of the six a picture drawn wrong, where software took about two hours. Three of the six were 2D cases that the card's compositing slowed, and the launch was changed that evening to give the card WebGL alone (`browser-launch.ts`'s own notes); the whole suite has not yet run on that launch.
- **Workers on the card, measured 2026-11-17** ([the record](../../../../AI-Agent-Workspace/AgentSessionHistory/2026/11/2026-11-17/2026-11-17.1.record.md#work-notes-after-turn-6-what-the-mount-costs-and-what-a-worker-costs)). The owner lifted the one-worker rule that day for "whatever the system reliably tolerates. I'm just looking to get our iteration speed up" ([Turn 4](../../../../AI-Agent-Workspace/AgentSessionHistory/2026/11/2026-11-17/2026-11-17.1.record.md#turn-4)). Counted from each process's own running total, a browser with its test keeps about 1.5 threads busy on the card (the page 0.76, the browser's graphics process 0.42) and 2.1 in software, on a box of ten cores. On sixteen 2D spec files (166 cases), from a checkout on the container's own disk: four workers finished in 7.4 minutes where one worker's cases take 16.1, every case 1.54 times as long as alone and four failed on waits that ran out; eight workers finished in 6.5 minutes, every case 2.3 times as long and twenty failed; eight in software, 8.1 minutes and thirty failed. Trace recording is about 8 % of a case at four workers. So the count stays at one in this file until a count is found that passes what one passes; what fails first under load is the framework's five seconds for a thing to become visible and the cases' own limits, which are the owner's alarms on some cases and plain waits on others, and telling the two apart is the work owed before the count is raised.
- **Two passes since 2026-11-18** ([what was measured](../../../../AI-Agent-Workspace/AgentSessionHistory/2026/11/2026-11-18/2026-11-18.1.record.md#work-notes-the-whole-suite-at-two-workers)). In the container on the Linux disk one worker ran the suite in 72 minutes (485 of 488) and two workers in 41.9 (482 of 488). Of the five cases red only at two workers, four ran out of a whole-case time limit that their own work nearly fills alone (`history-scrubber.spec.ts:391` and `:569` on the framework's 30 s, the layout lab's chain of this repository on the same, `local-map-branches.spec.ts:276` on its 60 s), and one read a picture before it was complete (`history-scrubber.spec.ts:180`, a fault to mend at its cause). So `npm run test:e2e` runs [`scripts/e2e-passes.mjs`](../../scripts/e2e-passes.mjs.mdmd.md): everything at two workers but the cases tagged `@alone`, then those at one worker. No limit was changed, which is how the plain waits were told from the alarms without touching either. `workers` here stays 1 for a direct run. `LIVE_DOCS_E2E_PASS` names the pass, and this file gives it its cases (`grepInvert` or `grep` on the tag), its workers, and for the second pass its own report and artifacts. The filter stands here rather than on the runner's command line because Playwright applies a configuration's filter together with a caller's `--grep`, where a second `--grep` on the command line replaces the first.
